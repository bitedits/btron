PART ONE -- THE NEXUS AND THE MACHINE

0. How a claim is stated in this book
-------------------------------------

  Every normative statement here has the same four parts, and a claim without
  all four is not a law but an intention:

  - LAW      what must hold, in one sentence, in the imperative or the
             indicative.
  - WHY      the mechanism that makes the alternative fail -- on this silicon,
             not in general.
  - MEASURE  the one decisive reading that says the law holds on the artifact
             that ships: a console row, a column of a row, or a comparison.
  - GATE     the assertion that goes red when the law is broken, and the
             control that proved it can go red.

Three reading rules bind the whole book.

  R1  A zero in a timing column means NOT INSTRUMENTED, never FREE.  On a host
      build every microsecond column reads 0 by construction, because
      btron_render_perf_us() is a weak `return 0` in src/graphics/dp_core.c
      and each platform core installs a strong clock.  Relations between
      columns are assertable off the device; absolute values are not.
  R2  Every quantity carries its label: PROVEN (measured on our own run, or
      read out of the register/packet table and reproduced by a gate) or
      INFERRED (believed from a datasheet, a default, or a secondary source,
      and therefore still a candidate).  A design decision taken on an
      inferred figure is stated as a bet, not as a measurement.
  R3  Counts and microseconds come from different builds.  The float census is
      compiled in with -DBTRON_PS2_FP_COUNT=1 and costs about 12 ms a frame,
      so no timing in this book comes from a counted image and no count comes
      from an uncounted one.

A claim has four parts; the page has two measures.

  Body prose is set at a two-space indent and breaks at column 78.  A record
  -- a table, a figure, a code excerpt, a dated console row -- keeps the
  columns it was authored with and may run wider: none in this book passes
  column 90.  A column that moves is a column that no longer lines up with the
  one above it, which is why records are not folded into the prose measure.
  Where a label and its text are split by a gutter, the gutter belongs to the
  label: LAW, WHY, MEASURE and GATE begin their text at the same column on
  every line of the block, and that is why the four-part shape above reads as
  a block rather than as hanging prose.

1. The thesis: one agent per parallel component
-----------------------------------------------

1.1  The substrate

  The InterCore nexus is the port's architecture, not a metaphor for it.
  ASYNC.txt §0.1 gives five primitives and one scheduling hook:

    pub [capacity]                create a writer cursor; yields a system-wide Id
    sub [publisher]               create a reader cursor bound to that writer
    spawn [core; program; cursors]  create a Task owning those cursors exclusively
    snd [writer; data]            non-blocking send
    rcv [reader]                  non-blocking receive

    poll_bus runs at every scheduler yield, so a message addressed to the
    current core is handled before its next task begins.  The topology is a
    star of single-producer rings: there is no broker, no central queue, no
    kernel trap, and no mutex anywhere on the path -- which is what removes
    priority inversion between the equal-priority planes (ASYNC.txt §0.1,
    INTERPRETER.txt §3.1).

  INTERPRETER.txt §3.2 states the refusal law that makes the topology safe:

    snd into a full ring   ->  S_FULL(drop_count)   the sender never blocks
    rcv on an empty port   ->  R_EMPTY              the receiver never blocks

  INTERPRETER.txt §3.0 partitions the work into four planes:

    1  Hardware and IRQ            3  Windowing and Presentation
    2  Real-Time Media/Streaming   4  Hypermedia Scripting

1.2  The law this book adds

  ASYNC.txt and INTERPRETER.txt describe a machine with N symmetric cores, so
  their theorem pins one worker per core.  A PlayStation 2 is not that
  machine: it is one scalar core plus five programmable engines that run
  concurrently and share one memory bus.  The correspondence therefore has to
  be stated stronger on this target than on a symmetric one:

  LAW 1 (one agent per parallel component).  On this machine there shall be a
  one-to-one correspondence between a nexus agent (a publisher with a ring and
  a period and a budget) and a programmable parallel component of the console.

  WHY  An engine with no agent is not idle hardware.  Nothing else can post to
       it, so its work has been silently reassigned to the EE, and the EE is
       the one component whose deadline is therefore charged with everyone
       else's work.  That reassignment -- not the arithmetic, not the bus --
       is why the presentation plane misses its budget by a factor of about 31
       (MEASURE 1.4), and it is the whole subject of Part Five.

1.4  What is broken, named

  VIOLATION 1 -- one publisher does four planes' work.  The EE is the port's
  entire agent topology: the desktop pass polls the bus, dispatches events,
  integrates the pointer, composites, rasterises when the GL backend is the
  CPU one, and uploads.  The measured line for it, on the GS build, is one
  period of 141,338 us that contains 123,355 us of composite and present and
  49,238 us of app paint, against the ASYNC_UI_BUDGET_US = 4000 the header
  declares (see 10 for the row and 16 for the lanes that close this).

  VIOLATION 2 -- the sender blocks.  ps2_gs_gif_send() in
  src/drivers/ps2/ps2_gs.c:112-113 is

        D2_CHCR = STR;
        while (D2_CHCR & (1 << 8)) ;

  a programmed-I/O start followed by a spin until the GIF has drained the
  packet.  §3.2 exists precisely so that no producer can be stopped by its
  consumer.  Two counters in this port already ARE §3.2 receipts -- drop=
  (gs_rec_dropped) and coverdrop= -- so the bounded-refusal half of the law
  has instruments; what has no instrument is the sender's own blocked time.
  Lane 10 is that instrument.

  VIOLATION 3 -- a period is not a deadline.  The µITRON shim is cooperative:
  sta_tsk() runs the task body on the caller's stack, stksz is ignored
  (core_ps2.c:4626-4646), and dly_tsk() is a CP0 Count busy-hold
  (ps2_task_hold(), :4729-4737).  A Tier-1 task with a period and a budget
  means something only when the kernel can take the CPU back at the boundary.
  The honest description of the current design is ONE PREEMPTION POINT PER
  FRAME, CHOSEN BY THE BODY; the budget misses above are misses the kernel is
  structurally unable to notice, never mind enforce.  Lane 11 is the fix.

1.5  The ratio that decides where work goes

  Three rows, one frame, one machine:

    what crosses                            px        EE time    ticks per px
    -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    the compositor's client-area blit    613,440      30,755 us        14.8
    the compositor's background restore  480,000       9,923 us         6.1
    the canvas's own GIF upload            480,000         ~823 us         0.51

  The upload figure is a subtraction, and the subtraction is justified by
  reading the two brackets: gs= is timed INSIDE upload=, so what the EE spends
  on a pixel it does not touch is the difference.

  A pixel the compositor touches costs the EE 14.8 cycles.  The same pixel
  crossing DMAC channel 2 into the same VRAM costs half a cycle.  That is a
  ~29x ratio measured inside one row pair of one frame, and it is the whole of
  Part Five:

    THE QUESTION THIS BOOK EXISTS TO ANSWER IS NOT "HOW DO WE MAKE THE EE
    FASTER" BUT "WHY IS THE EE TOUCHING PIXELS AT ALL".

  The only agent that can fill a rectangle of VRAM without the EE touching it
  is the GS, and it can do so two ways: a SPRITE (RECT) primitive for a solid
  or texture-mapped region, and a BITBLTBUF/TRXPOS/TRXREG/TRXDIR(2)
  Local-to-Local transfer for a copy that stays inside VRAM.  Both encoders
  already exist and are already host-gated; neither has had a caller that
  blits until lane 9.

  Emulator note, without letting it drive the design.  PCSX2 runs the IOP and,
  with MTVU, VU1 on separate host threads, so on THIS machine work handed to
  those engines is parallelism the wall clock can bank.  That is a reason to
  build the agents, not a reason to build them emulator-shaped: every engine
  in the table is real silicon on a real console, the SIF and VIF paths are
  the same packet protocols there, and the only row whose NUMBERS are
  emulator-dependent is the one chapter 8 owns.

2. The machine, as six agents on one bus
----------------------------------------

  LAW 2 (the ledger is the design).  Every programmable parallel component of
  the console shall appear in this ledger with four answers: which nexus plane
  it belongs to, whether an agent drives it today, what its period and budget
  are, and what its work costs here.  A component whose cost is "zero
  instructions in the image" and whose agent is "absent" is a lane, not a
  footnote.

  WHY  A budget is only meaningful against a component that can spend it.  The
       port has one set of periods and one set of budgets, and the ledger is
       what says which of them the machine can actually honour -- see 1.4's
       third violation.

  Read each block as one row of the port's agent topology.  The label after
  the cost is PROVEN or INFERRED in the sense of rule R2.

2.1  EE -- MIPS R5900 scalar core + COP1
       plane      1, 3 and 4 all at once

                  (sta_tsk nests the body on the caller's stack)
       period     whatever the body's dly_tsk() asked for

       crosses    nothing -- it is both writer and reader of every stage of a
                  frame
       cost       composite 56,128 us + app 46,273 us + present 1,031 us, in
                  one period of 149,308 us PROVEN (three console rows of one
                  image: [BENCH] compositor:, [XMBT] frame, [XMBT] cycle).
                  Re-read 2026-10-13 on the keyboard build: period=149,304 us,
                  upload=1,031 us IDENTICAL, app 46,275 us -- so this is a
                  steady state and not one lucky frame, and the two dates
                  agree to 4 us on a 149 ms period.)

       plane      1 -- Hardware and IRQ

3. The plane table: periods and budgets belong to the machine
-------------------------------------------------------------

  LAW 3 (one record).  Every period this machine presents, integrates and ages
  against lives in ONE record -- btron_plane_cfg_t in include/btron/async_rt.h
  -- installed by btron_planes_install() and read through btron_planes() in
  src/graphics/dp_core.c.  No consumer shall hold its own copy of a period.

  WHY  A period is not a property of whichever rasteriser the image happens to
       link.  On this port the pass that paints is also the pass that spends
       the pointer's distance, so every "pixels per pass" the cursor is tuned
       with is the frame time in disguise -- and the divisor that turns a
       period into clock ticks is the machine's own measurement, for the
       reason given in chapter 8.

  MEASURE  the machine states its own periods as the first row after SIO comes
           up:

    [PLANE] input=1000 us  ui=16667 us  panel=200000 us  converge=2000000 us
            slices=16

  GATE   ps2_planes_install() is step 1a of ps2_kernel_main(), immediately
         after ps2_sio_init() and before the timebase probe, the OHCI probe,
         the shell loop and the desktop session -- which is why the readers
         below take the table as given instead of testing it for NULL.  A
         missing table is a BOOT-ORDER BUG; self-pacing is the arm64 port's
         business, not this one's.

  The five fields, each with the consumer that reads it and the label its
  claim carries:

  input_period_us = 1000 us  --  PROVEN
       ps2_ptr_input_slices(): what one pass may spend is PS2_PTR_MAX_STEP
       times the 1 ms periods that actually elapsed since the last service.
       The clock behind it is hardware's own: HcFmInterval is a 1 ms OHCI
       frame and ps2_usb_frame_number() is that clock.

  ui_period_us = 16667 us  --  INFERRED
       NOTHING YET reads it -- it is the absolute deadline the async UI plane
       is installed against, so it is in the table before it has a reader.
       The number is inferred from the programmed mode (800x600 inside
       720p/60).

  panel_refresh_us = 200000 us  --  THIS IS A CHOICE, not a measurement
       ps2_gui_pass(): the system panel's 28 rows join the band set on this
       clock even with no damage.

  full_convergence_us = 2000000 us  --  convergence, not animation
       ps2_gui_pass(): the whole-canvas force_full net.

  input_slices_max = 16
       The clamp on the slice count: one 60 Hz frame of catch-up.  It bounds
       the WORK, not the debt, so a pass returning from a 683 ms XMB frame
       costs one pass.

  The ui_period_us field is the port's own inconsistency, stated plainly here
  rather than smoothed over: the header declares ASYNC_UI_PERIOD_US = 8333
  while the table installs 16667, and neither has a reader.  Appendix B
  carries it as misalignment M1 and lane 11 as the row that closes it.

  With the shipped pointer cap off (PS2_PTR_MAX_STEP 0) the product fed to
  dp_ptr_limit() is zero, and a non-positive limit means no limit -- so the
  slicing is the identity exactly when the limiter is, and nothing about the
  shipped, proportional pointer changes.  What changes is what `maxstep N`
  MEANS: N pixels per millisecond rather than N pixels per pass, which at the
  measured 259 passes/s is a little under 4x more travel for the same number.
  That is the difference the loopback calibrator has to be tuned against
  (13.2).

=====================================================================
PART TWO -- THE LINK: LAWS THAT MAKE THE IMAGE LEGAL
=====================================================================

  These four chapters are not style notes.  Each one exists because a bug
  reached a screen, and none of the four can be seen by the code that causes
  it: an image that breaks them boots, prints timings, and is subtly wrong.
  All four are laws about the LINK, which is why they come before any
  discussion of what the port does.

4. The Image Budget
-------------------

  LAW 4 (one ceiling).  The whole image -- .text, .data, .bss -- shall end
  below 0x01FF0000, the stack pointer boot_ps2.s installs.  That is the real
  limit rather than the 0x02000000 end of RDRAM, because .bss grows upward
  into the stack.

  WHY  Statics the link places past the ceiling are not an error at runtime.
       RDRAM simply is not there, so a store to one is swallowed and the load
       returns garbage.  The failure is silent and PER OBJECT: snd_evt() keeps
       returning E_OK while its g_q_count++ goes nowhere, and get_evt()
       answers E_TMOUT forever.  That is why the symptom reads as "the event
       system is broken" and never as out of memory.  A .bss that overruns
       also takes the deskbar (g_gmenu), the start menu (g_tracker), the
       compositor's background cache and the fs/vobj tables with it, while
       pointer-path statics that section order happened to place low keep
       working -- so the report is a partial desktop, not a crash.

  MEASURE  the image on disk, re-read 2026-10-13 under the dials its [CFG] row
           names (PS2_GL=eegs PS2_FP=hw): ELF 5,967,796 bytes, _end =
           0x01F06600, headroom 956,928 bytes (0.91 MiB) below the ceiling.
           .data is 0x414c00 bytes and its last 1 MiB is the embedded /SYS
           volume (20.1), so the volume's own size is what _end grew by -- and
           doc/md/PS2.md, the handbook this book was cast from, IS one of the
           volume's 33 records, so editing that prose rebuilds the volume and
           the image around it.  A size therefore belongs with its date, not
           with a configuration: 5,966,580 bytes at _end = 0x01F06200 was an
           earlier reading on the same day, and 5,967,604 bytes was
           2026-10-12's.  The two readings on the 13th differ in _end by
           EXACTLY 1,024 bytes -- one whole block of the volume's block size
           -- which is this law demonstrating itself: the handbook was edited,
           the volume gained a block, and the image grew around it.  This
           book's own text is NOT a volume payload, and the guest cannot read
           it: /SYS carries the handbook.

  GATE   src/drivers/ps2/ps2.ld enforces the budget with a link-time ASSERT,
         so a source list that grows the image past RAM fails at the link
         instead of on the guest.  The budget is why PS2_SRCS carries
         PS2_GL_SRCS (the GL layer plus xmb.c and glgears.c) rather than
         GL_SRCS, whose QUAKE_SRCS and DEMO_SRCS alone account for roughly 82
         MB of .bss -- the 64 MB Quake hunk arena and the demo pack's arrays.

5. One Calling Convention
-------------------------

  LAW 5 (one ISA, one FP ABI).  Every C translation unit in btron-ps2.elf is
  built -march=mips2 -mabi=32 -msoft-float.  The image contains no second ISA
  of C and no second floating-point ABI.

  WHY  This is a rule about the LINK, not about instruction speed.  Under O32,
       a 64-bit-GPR MIPS (what -march=mips3 selects) passes fixed arguments
       5..8 in $t0..$t3 where a 32-bit-GPR MIPS passes them on the stack, and
       it lays its vararg save area out in 8-byte slots while va_arg still
       walks four bytes at a time.  Each convention is self-consistent inside
       one translation unit, so a mixed link produces no warning, no bad
       instruction and no crash -- only the fifth and later argument of a
       cross-file call arrives as garbage, and every %s in a console line
       printed from the other side of the boundary shifts the rest of the line
       by a word.

  The cursor reported this law on 2026-09-24 and again on 2026-10-09.
  ps2_paint_region() in src/cores/core_ps2.c appends the sprite with
  draw_baremetal_mouse_cursor(screen, x, y, w, h), a five-argument call into
  src/desktop/desktop.c.  The two files were compiled for different ISAs, so h
  -- the canvas height -- was junk, every row of the draw failed `py >= h`,
  and the sprite vanished on the first banded pass after the whole-canvas
  repaint that draws it from inside desktop.c, where the call stays within one
  ISA.  A pointer that appears only every two seconds, on the convergence
  repaint, IS this failure.

  Why mips2 is the side that wins, and the exceptions it needs:

  - -march=mips3 cannot be the uniform choice because its vararg layout is the
    one va_arg disagrees with, and the port's console (ps2_kprintf,
    tkl_vsnprintf) is variadic on every platform.
  - -march=mips32r2 would be 32-bit-GPR too, but clang may reach for ext, ins,
    clz and madd, which a R5900 does not implement.  mips2's instruction set
    is a subset of the Emotion Engine's MIPS III.
  - The GS latches a privileged register on a SINGLE 64-BIT STORE, which a
    32-bit-GPR build cannot emit.  src/drivers/ps2/ps2_gs_reg.s provides
    ps2_gs_poke64() and ps2_gs_peek64(), and C reaches them through
    GS_REG_POKE / GS_REG_PEEK with three 32-bit arguments -- an interface
    identical under either ISA, which is what makes it a safe place for an
    assembly-only exception.  ps2_halt's `wait` in boot_ps2.s is enabled the
    same way, with .set push / .set mips3 around the one instruction.
  - Converting a double to or from a 64-bit integer has no instruction on this
    CPU either -- that is chapter 6's subject, and ps2_builtins.c exists to
    satisfy it.

  MEASURE  the cursor phase of scripts/ps2_bench.sh: a banded pass must report
           `sig at drawn: bb=1 fb=1` with `pre=0 post=1` per region -- the
           append ran and wrote, in the backbuffer and in the presented frame
           buffer.  post=0 while appended=1 is a five-argument call whose
           fifth argument did not arrive, and the phase's verdict names that
           case.

  GATE   scripts/test_ps2.sh holds the static side: the linked image's MIPS
         ABI flags read ISA: MIPS2 / GPR size: 32; NO *.ps2.o reports 64-bit
         GPRs (a stale ELF can outlive a flag change -- see 21.2);
         ps2_gs_poke64 contains exactly one `sd` and ps2_gs_peek64 exactly one
         `ld`.

6. The FPU Law, and the register file inside it
-----------------------------------------------

  This is the longest law in the port, because it has three layers and each
  layer was learned separately: what instructions the CPU answers (6.1), how
  arguments travel (6.2), and which registers a unit may hold (6.3).  A
  violation of the first is a black screen, of the second a wrong argument, of
  the third a silent corruption of someone else's value.

6.1  Single precision only, and an illegal op that does nothing

  LAW 6a.  The Emotion Engine's FPU is single-precision.  ldc1, sdc1 and every
  other double-precision COP1 instruction are not merely slow on a R5900 --
  they are ILLEGAL, and PCSX2's reaction to one is to print `Unknown R5900
  COP1:` and let the instruction DO NOTHING.

  WHY  Nothing is worse than a trap here.  The load leaves the destination
       register at whatever it held; the store lands nowhere; the program
       continues with a double that is silently a previous double.  Every
       float-heavy file in this port was written in double, so before this law
       the XMB reported black=383999 other=1 out of 384000 pixels and one
       bench run printed 2,093 `Unknown R5900` rows.

  The rule therefore binds the WHOLE IMAGE, not the two conversions that
  happened to need libcalls: PS2_CC carries -msoft-float, so a translation
  unit emits no COP1 instruction and FP arithmetic reaches the runtime as a
  function call.

  Three units, and only three, are built -mhard-float by design and only for
  single precision (Makefile PS2_FP ?= hw; PS2_FP=soft builds all of them
  without it):

    src/drivers/ps2/ps2_builtins.c   the soft-float runtime's single-precision
                                     entries
    src/gl/tinygl/vertex_stage.c     the geometry kernel: takes a GLVertex*,
                                     returns nothing, so its floats never
                                     leave the unit
    src/apps/xmb_wave.c              the wave's lattice: takes the effect
                                     clock as IEEE-754 BITS in an integer,
                                     takes the window size as two integers,
                                     returns nothing, and hands the sheet out
                                     through memory the reader loads as float

  Each is permitted by the second layer rather than exempt from it: a float
  shall never cross one of those three boundaries.  Under -msoft-float clang
  rejects `mtc1` with "instruction requires a CPU feature not currently
  enabled", which is why the EE's own FPU was unusable for single precision
  until the first file, unusable for the geometry until the second, and
  unusable for the wave's lattice until the third.  The link still records `FP
  ABI: Soft float` for the whole image, and mipsel-linux-gnu-ld accepts the
  mixed .MIPS.abiflags the way it is.  PS2_FP=soft is the escape hatch if a
  console ever boots with the FPU off, since a hard-float unit cannot ask the
  runtime valve (btron_fp_on) to substitute for instructions it has already
  emitted.

6.2  Mixing is an ABI law, not a performance note

  LAW 6b.  One file built without -msoft-float shall never hand a float to a
  callee built with it, or receive one from it.

  WHY  Under -msoft-float, O32 passes floating values in INTEGER registers: a
       double occupies an even/odd GPR pair with the low word in the even
       register, so the first two doubles are $4:$5 and $6:$7 and a third
       spills to the stack; a float takes one register; a double returns in
       $2:$3.  Those are exactly the slots the FPU ABI would fill with COP1
       registers, so the callee's arguments stay untouched.  This was read off
       a disassembly of this toolchain, not off a manual.

  MEASURE / GATE  ps2_builtins.c supplies the 32 libcalls that link then
                  requires, because -nostdlib provides none and this machine
                  has no libgcc or compiler-rt for the target.  It is written
                  against that measured ABI -- each entry is declared with
                  uint64_t/uint32_t arguments, register-for-register identical
                  to the double/float form clang expects -- and implements
                  exact round-to-nearest-even IEEE-754 by hand.  Two details
                  carry the correctness:

  - Every 64-bit shift and split is expressed with 32-bit variable shifts plus
    constant 64-bit shifts, so the file never references __lshrdi3, __udivdi3
    or __muldi3.  It must be self-contained or it defines the problem it
    solves.
  - A comparison libcall returns -1/0/1 and the CALLER decides the meaning: lt
    tests < 0, le tests < 1, ge tests > -1, gt tests > 0.  All four share one
    three-way routine, which returns the unordered case as +1 -- the only
    value that makes lt, le, eq and ne all behave for a NaN.  gt and ge need
    unordered -1 and so call the same routine with that argument.

  The validation is `make test-ps2-softfloat`: it includes the implementation
  textually and runs it against the host's own FPU over 18,384,493 comparisons
  (re-read 2026-10-13; the count moves with the suite, `failures=0` does not)
  -- random bit patterns mixed with special values, a power-of-two sweep,
  named ties, subnormal cancellation, overflow, and a 3,072-case sweep over
  every exponent field for the truncate -- and asserts the results are
  BIT-IDENTICAL.  Three deliberate mutations, each with the clean run re-gated
  between them: deleting the exponent increment on the rounding carry-out
  reports 1,488 failures and names `thirds` and `truncdfsf2`; taking the
  32-bit windowing off the double multiply's middle word reports 450,068 and
  names `muldf3`; both re-run 2026-10-13, one control per command.  The third,
  a saturation line moved from exponent 158 to 159, was measured when the
  truncate was rewritten and reported 694.  That is what makes the passing
  number mean something rather than prove nothing.

6.3  The register file inside the law

  LAW 6c (the callee-saved floats do not exist).  A hard-float unit of this
  port shall hold no float across no call at all, and the preference that
  would put one there is priced out of the build.

  WHY  The R5900 has 32 SINGLE-precision registers and no double ones.  So the
       O32 convention's callee-saved $f20/$f22/.../$f30 -- which a normal MIPS
       preserves with an sdc1 of the even/odd pair -- cannot be preserved here
       at all: the store and its reload are both ignored, and a unit that lets
       the allocator put a live value in one of them silently destroys its
       caller's.  Two of the three ways that happens are visible in the source
       (a working set over the caller-saved ten; a loop whose invariant float
       is live across its own call).  The third is LLVM's allocator PREFERRING
       a callee-saved register for any long live range, which is a cost-model
       decision no source spelling changes.  Measured during row 7: a kernel
       doing `jal __fixsfsi` nine times gained two sdc1 in its prologue and
       two ldc1 in its epilogue -- four double-width FP accesses no line of
       source asked for.

  MEASURE  the three units are built with -mllvm
           -regalloc-csr-first-time-cost=1000000000 (PS2_FP_CSR_COST), and the
           image is then scanned for the pair instructions.  On the wave unit
           this flag is 52 pair stores down to 4, and those four were one real
           working-set overflow that the source has since been split for.  On
           the source as it now stands, dropping the flag gives 14 pair stores
           and 32 references to $f20-$f31 against the shipped object's ZERO of
           each -- the control that says the knob is still load-bearing rather
           than a relic.  An unknown -mllvm argument fails the compile, so a
           toolchain that drops the option stops the build instead of quietly
           re-opening the hole.

  GATE   the image's FP rows: no object except the three named units contains
         an FP instruction at all (a leftover .s op elsewhere means a TU that
         escaped -msoft-float, and its arguments would arrive in registers the
         rest of the image does not use); the image contains no double-width
         op and neither of the two single-precision encodings this CPU does
         not implement; those encodings are absent BYTE FOR BYTE as well,
         because a mnemonic grep trusts the disassembler's naming; the three
         sanctioned units really do emit COP1 arithmetic, so a stale
         soft-float build cannot pass by having no FPU code; no named symbol
         is undefined; all 32 libcalls are present.

6.4  The compares the EE does not have

  LAW 6d.  Inside a -mhard-float translation unit of this port, a float
  comparison is written with the EVEN encoding or not at all.

  WHY  The R5900's COP1 decoder answers c.olt.s (0x34), c.ole.s (0x36),
       c.oeq.s (0x32) and the two constant forms.  It does NOT answer the
       odd-funct, unordered-inclusive family c.un.s (0x31), c.ueq.s (0x33),
       c.ult.s (0x35), c.ule.s (0x37).  An unrecognised compare is the worst
       failure mode this CPU has: PCSX2 neither traps nor stalls it, and the
       condition code keeps whatever the PREVIOUS compare wrote, so the branch
       that follows goes the way the last comparison decided.

  This is what the shipped image did to the XMB.  gl_clipcode()
  (src/gl/tinygl/zgl.h) tests a vertex against the frustum's six planes as
  three `x < -w` and three `w < x`.  Under -mhard-float clang lowers the first
  kind to c.olt.s and the second to c.ule.s -- ordered greater is
  `not(unordered or less-or-equal)`, so the natural encoding for a > b is the
  unordered-inclusive one with the branches inverted.  Three of the six plane
  tests therefore read a stale flag.  The faces that came back clipped were
  exactly the ones inside every plane; the arithmetic beside them was provably
  right; and the census reported the truth of it:

                        before                    after
    faces seen/left/drawn  5406 / 5406 / 0         5614 / 210 / 4942
    OR of clip codes       0x2f (five planes)      0x7  (three, the real ones)
    XMB census             black=384000            black=0 other=384000

  clipor's missing ZMIN bit is the whole diagnosis in one digit: the three
  c.olt.s answered correctly, the three c.ule.s did not, and no triangle ever
  reached a fill -- a black screen with a working rasteriser, a correct ortho
  and correct matrices.

  MEASURE / GATE  src/drivers/ps2/ps2_fp.h provides ps2_fp_olt_s(a, b) --
                  mtc1, mtc1, one nop for the FPU to see the new operands,
                  c.olt.s, one nop because the condition code is written one
                  cycle after the compare retires, bc1f with a nop in its
                  delay slot -- and zgl.h routes the six clip tests through
                  TGL_CLIP_LT(), which is that helper exactly when the file is
                  compiled for the EE's FPU and plain C otherwise.  ORDERED
                  MEANS ORDERED: a NaN pair makes c.olt.s false, which is what
                  `a < b` means in C, so hw and soft cannot diverge here.
                  Three image rows hold it: a mnemonic sweep carrying
                  c.(un|ueq|ult|ule).s next to trunc.w.s; the same law read
                  byte-for-byte over .text, one ALIGNED INSTRUCTION WORD per
                  line, matching funct 0x31/33/35/37 inside a 0x46 COP1
                  compare (an unaligned hex-string grep of the same family
                  reports 22 hits on a clean image, which is why the scan is
                  word-anchored); and a row reading the geometry kernel
                  directly for at least six c.olt.s and none of the family, so
                  the fix cannot be satisfied by a file that contains no
                  compares at all.

6.5  What the FPU is, measured

  btron_fp_init() enables CU1 if the BIOS left it clear, masks the FPU's
  exception enables, and proves the unit answers with one round trip (1.0f +
  2.0f must come back 0x40400000); the result is recorded in btron_fp_on, so a
  machine whose FPU does not answer falls back to the bit engine and costs
  cycles rather than pixels.  Doubles never go to the FPU at all, because it
  has no double-precision instruction.  btron_fp_selftest() runs both engines
  over the same operand vector and prints the verdict as [FPU] rows:

    Question                         Answer
    -----------------------------------------------------------------
    one op, inside a hard-float TU   COP1 20 cycles, bit engine 160 -- 8x
    ...from a -msoft-float caller    COP1 28 cycles, bit engine 536 (28 = 20 +
                                     the jal and the operand moves; 536 is the
                                     generic engine's entry work too)
    where do they disagree?          add.s, sub.s, mul.s only.  div.s, sqrt.s,
                                     the five compares and cvt.s.w agree on
                                     every pair
    how far?                         exactly one representable step, never
                                     wider, never across zero -- 61 of 576
                                     clean pair-runs
    which engine is right?           the bit engine: it is host-proven exact,
                                     so each of those 61 is COP1 answering the
                                     neighbour that is NOT correctly rounded
    is it a rounding mode?           no.  Those classes make a rounding
                                     decision on 60/58/42 of their pairs and
                                     COP1 is wrong on 28/14/19, while div.s --
                                     which decides on 81, more than any other
                                     op -- is wrong on none.  A mode cannot
                                     skip the op with the most decisions
    flush-to-zero for subnormals?    no: that count is zero

  So the gates on the arithmetic are fpu_gap_wide == 0 and fpu_gap_sign == 0,
  NOT "agrees with the bit engine": a one-step gap is invisible in an 8-bit
  pixel, and a demand that add.s be IEEE-exact is a demand that this CPU be a
  different CPU.

  28 is the price every stage table in this book bills a float operation at,
  and it is the one number that says whether an arithmetic change is worth
  making.  The other three prices the census uses: float -> integer 47 cycles,
  integer -> float 23, a double 536.

  What no measurement in this tree can settle: every [FPU] number here came
  from PCSX2's Emotion EE.  The same rows print over SIO on a real console, so
  the identical image run there says whether the one-step residue is the
  emulator or the silicon.

7. One canvas, one byte order, one alpha
----------------------------------------

  LAW 7a (the swap is a leaf).  A BTRON COLOR is 0xAARRGGBB, whose
  little-endian bytes are B,G,R,A; a GS CT32 pixel is R,G,B,A in memory.
  Neither the GIF's Host-to-Local bit transfer nor the PCRTC's display read
  can permute channels, so the exchange lives in the port -- and it lives at
  the moment each colour ENTERS a pixel array.  BTRON_PIXEL_FB() in
  include/btron/types.h swaps the R and B fields, is its own inverse, and is
  the identity on every non-PS2 target.

  WHY  The rule is closed under copying.  A pixel array written through it and
       read back only as words -- the compositor's spans, row blits,
       background cache, cursor save area, and the GIF upload itself -- needs
       nothing.  That is why the conversion is a leaf store and a
       component-reader detail rather than a pass over the canvas.  The pass
       it replaced touched 480,000 words per present, 49,094 us of a 274 ms
       frame.

  LAW 7b (alpha travels with the pixel).  Every pixel of a GL surface shall
  arrive with 0xFF on top, and it shall get it AT THE STORE, never in a walk
  afterwards.  The law is in src/gl/tinygl/zbuffer.h:

  - FB_PIXEL(v) ORs the constant alpha into a pixel and is used at
    FRAME-BUFFER stores only -- clear, the flat, interpolated, textured and
    both blended triangle paths, lines and points, glDrawPixels, the glyph
    blit, the postprocess, and every TGL_*_BLEND_FUNC arm.  It is idempotent,
    so a pixel that already carries the byte is
    unchanged.
  - It is NEVER inside RGB_TO_PIXEL, because the same macro builds texels and
    a texel's top byte IS its alpha (TGL_TEX_ALPHA), which the blend
    multiplies by.  Forcing a texel opaque turns every icon and glyph in an
    atlas into a solid square.
  - Nothing reads a frame buffer's alpha back: GL_DST_ALPHA has no arm in
    TGL_BLEND_FACTOR because a canvas has no alpha to read.
  - The pixels no primitive covers are stamped once, when the array is new:
    tinygl_backend_init() and a tinygl_backend_resize() that ACTUALLY changed
    the buffer or its size.  The guard matters -- egl_make_current() routes
    through the resize path every time a context is made current, and a walk
    there would be the per-frame pass this replaced.
  - With nothing left to do, the TinyGL backend's swap_buffers slot is NULL.

  WHY  The walk used to do one uncached read and one uncached write per pixel
       per frame, measured at swap=10498 us for the XMB's 384,000 px -- 10.5
       ms of a 184 ms frame with no arithmetic in it at all.

  MEASURE  swap= reads 0 on every frame row, and the present's swap stage is
           0.  The smoothness gate xswap_us/full_swap_us passes at its
           "target: absent" budget.

  GATE   `make test-ps2-fb-order`: verify/tests/test_fb_order.c renders one
         frame that covers every store path above and asserts that both GL
         canvases hold ZERO non-opaque words, in each of the two byte-order
         domains.  It was shown to discriminate -- removing FB_PIXEL from the
         recognised-blend pack alone reports "2158 non-opaque words of 6144
         drawn" before the restore.  The XMB's PPMs cannot see this change at
         all (they carry RGB), which is why the alpha has its own gate rather
         than riding on the frame comparison.  The same suite's two binaries
         normalise to the same 31,488 words, which is the first direct
         evidence that linking the GS stage changes nothing the canvas holds.

PART THREE -- THE MEASURED MACHINE
=====================================================================

  Part Two said what the image must be.  This Part says what it costs, in
  units that can be checked against a row of log, and fixes the clock those
  rows are read on.  Nothing here is an estimate: each price was fitted or
  bracketed, and each row that prints one is named.

8. The measured microsecond
---------------------------

  LAW 8 (one divisor, measured per machine).  A single function converts
  microseconds into CP0 Count ticks for the whole image --
  btron_ee_ticks_per_us() in src/drivers/ps2/ps2_irq.c -- and the boot
  MEASURES it instead of quoting the core clock.  No other file shall hold an
  opinion about what a microsecond is.

  WHY  Four copies of the datasheet figure used to be in the tree:
       EE_TICKS_PER_US in core_ps2.c (ps2_us_since(), the stage split, the
       pointer's slices, the panel and convergence nets, the task hold), one
       in ps2_irq.c, EE_COUNTS_PER_US in ps2_usb.c and another in
       ps2_iopram.c.  Each was an independent opinion, and on this emulator
       all four were wrong by exactly 2x -- so every microsecond this image
       printed was twice as long as it said.

  MEASURE  the instrument is the INPUT plane itself.  Timer0 (T0_COUNT
           0xB0000000, T0_MODE 0xB0000010, T0_COMP 0xB0000020, all through the
           uncached KSEG1 alias) is programmed CLKS=2 (bus clock / 256) with
           COMP = 576, which is the plane table's input_period_us = 1000 at
           the DOCUMENTED rate.  It is installed through the ROM kernel's own
           interrupt registry rather than by taking the exception vector:
           AddIntcHandler (syscall 0x10) for INTC_TIM0 = 9, then EnableIntc
           (0x14), so the BIOS dispatcher still owns vectoring.  The mode word
           written each period is 0x1c2 -- CLKS=2 | ZRET | CUE | CMPE |
           EQUF|OVFF ack.  ZRET makes the period self-repeating, and because
           the compare flag LATCHES, the handler must re-write that same word
           to acknowledge it: without the acknowledge the machine delivered
           exactly one tick ever and then sat there looking armed.

  THE CALIBRATION LAW.  On each entry the handler takes the gap since the
  previous entry and sums the first CALIB_PERIODS = 32 of them; when the 32nd
  lands it divides by periods x input_period_us = 32,000 real microseconds,
  rounding half up, and publishes the quotient.  The window closes IN the
  handler because only the entries know the spacing; the ISR budget is
  re-priced in the same breath, since the periods that filled the window had
  been charged against the documented divisor.  Zero is the "not yet measured"
  sentinel, so a machine whose Count does not advance cannot report a
  measurement and keeps the fallback EE_TICKS_PER_US_DOC = 147.
  ps2_irq_calib_wait() bounds its patience in SPINS, not in microseconds -- a
  timeout written in the unit being measured would be circular -- and boot
  step 2a runs it before the first thing the image times with the result.

    Quantity                                          Status
    ------------------------------------------------------------------
    295 ticks per microsecond on this emulator         MEASURED: 9,437,184 ticks
                                                        summed over 32 periods
    Count runs at the CORE rate (294.912 MHz), not     MEASURED: the mean gap is
    the documented half of it                           294,912 per 1,000 us to the
                                                        digit
    The plane and the OHCI agree: 50 Timer0 periods    MEASURED: two independent
    in a window the frame counter also calls 50        hardware clocks; 147 was the
                                                        outlier of the three
    ~147 on a real console (hence the fallback)        INFERRED from the datasheet;
                                                        the same code measures it
                                                        there, so the row will say
                                                        which it got

  GATE   two image rows: every mtc0 in the image is a Status write, and NONE
         names register 9 -- Count is read, never set, and writing it would
         break every elapsed time in the image.  scripts/ps2_smooth.sh takes
         ee_ticks_per_us as [INFO] (it is the machine's business, not the
         port's) and judges input_plane_short_us and ohci_frame_short_us at a
         target of 0.

  Everything stays 32-bit.  The suite forbids a named undefined symbol, so a
  __muldi3 or __udivdi3 from a stray 64-bit product kills the image at link
  time.  Therefore the wrap-fold's step is 0xFFFFFFFF / tpu (about 14.56 ms of
  Count at 295), the panel and convergence nets fit plainly, and a task hold
  budget is capped at 250 ms rather than widened.

  The handler is integer-only, by constraint and not by taste: no exception
  vector is installed, so the ROM kernel saves the GPRs and NOT the FPRs
  before it reaches this handler.  With COP1 live, a handler that touched a
  float would corrupt whatever the interrupted code had in those registers.
  The same reason makes ps2_ee_exit_handler() a `sync` plus a hand-written
  Status read-modify-write: `ei` (0x41606020) is not decoded by this PCSX2's
  R5900 -- it prints `Unknown R5900 COP0` and does nothing -- so the EE's
  global enable is bit 16, EIE, and it is raised with li/or/mtc0 $12.

  The boot prints the whole chain, and each row exists to falsify a different
  claim:

    [ASYNC] input plane Timer0 (INTC 9): period=1000 us compare=576 ticks=50 of
            the 50 this 50000 us window owes -> LIVE
    [ASYNC] hid collection: the input plane reads the OHCI rings and a pass
            delivers them
    [ASYNC] machine microsecond = 295 Count ticks, measured over 32 periods of the
            plane (9437184 ticks summed, mean 294912)
    [ASYNC] gaps since the arm: 9881..4118890 ticks = 33..13962 us, against the
            1000 us the period means
    [ASYNC] the same window on the OHCI's frame counter: 50 frames of the 50 a 1 ms
            frame says this is -- agree
    [ASYNC] CP0.Status 0x70030c11 at the arm, 0x70030c11 after it: EIE(bit16)=1 -> 1
    [ASYNC] timer0 MODE=0x000001c2 (clk256+zret+cue+cmpe+ack) count 102 -> 102 over
            a 2 ms hold, compare=576, ticks=238 total
    [ASYNC] isr wcet=498 ticks = 1.68 us of the 50 us budget, overruns=0
    [CPU]   CP0 Count running (295 ticks/us, measured against the input plane)

  LIVE is `got + 1 >= owed`, not "at least one tick": a plane that delivered 3
  of the 50 periods it owes is STALLED, and one that delivered none is DEAD.
  The gaps print in raw ticks AND microseconds for a reason: the mean gap
  divided by the divisor IS the period by construction, so the microsecond
  form of the calibration's own sample carries no information -- and the
  accessors that used to offer it are gone.

  The one instrument that does NOT work here, measured.  A plain read of
  T0_COUNT barely advances on this emulator: over a hold that is two periods
  of COMP=576 -- 1152 counts -- the delta was 140 -> 140, 276 -> 277, 551 ->
  551, while the same timer's compare interrupt arrived 50 times in the same
  50 ms.  INFERRED: PCSX2 keeps the timer's schedule somewhere other than that
  word, so the readable counter is a residue, and with ZRET a sample taken a
  period apart aliases any phase difference to zero.  The tick count and the
  OHCI row are therefore the cadence evidence; the MODE word is the part of
  that row worth reading.

9. The instruments: which row answers which question
----------------------------------------------------

  LAW 9 (one question, one row).  Every claim this book makes is answerable by
  one named row, and each row's columns are owned by exactly one layer.
  Before a measurement is taken, the question is stated together with the row
  that answers it and the row that does not.

  R1 and R3 from chapter 0 apply to every one of these rows; three more
  reading rules belong to the instruments themselves:

  - gs= IS NOT THE GS'S TIME.  It is the EE's send-and-wait bracket, timed
    inside upload= (core_ps2.c:1147-51).  Nothing in this image measures GS
    raster time, and 2.5's row says so rather than leaving the column to be
    read as the engine's cost.
  - The compositor columns are MAXIMA, not this frame's exclusive cost.
    btron_render_stats_take() runs at the stamp, so a comp column is the
    largest value the compositor recorded since the previous profiled pass.
    With the app's whole-canvas composite at 112-126 ms and the intervening
    session passes painting bands, the app's frame IS that maximum here; a
    bench that profiled a quiet frame would not be reading what it names.
  - A row behind a build dial belongs to that dial.  The census is compiled
    with -DBTRON_PS2_FP_COUNT=1 and costs ~12.4 ms a frame; the float census
    and the microseconds therefore come from different images, always (rule
    R3).

10. What a pixel costs
----------------------

  LAW 10 (price per unit of work, not per stage).  Every raster cost claim
  shall be attributable to ONE billable unit -- a pixel, a span step, a
  triangle setup, a vertex, a call -- and the unit's price shall be
  reproducible from rows in one log.

  WHY  A stage table says where time went; it cannot say WHAT was billed, and
       a plan built on stages spends effort on the wrong term.  The prices
       below came from fitting rows of the same image against each other,
       which is the only method that separates a per-pixel cost from a
       per-triangle one.  Both mesh rows in the fit come from ONE binary: a
       pair of rows from two different images prices whatever changed between
       them, not the mesh.

  MEASURE  reproduce with `SMOOTH_APP=1 SMOOTH_FRAMES=3 SMOOTH_IDLE=20

  raster stages, f_ribbon is 38% float and f_menu 30%; f_bg is 0.3%, i.e. a
  pure pixel cost.

  CONSEQUENCES, all four normative for where work goes:

  1. THE FLOAT LAYER'S CEILING WAS 1.51x, AND THE GS RETIRED IT.  Deleting
     every one of the 578,963 calls -- an infinite-speed FPU -- took the draw
     from 173.8 ms to 115.0 ms, and the frame a person needs is 16.7 ms.  With
     the fill on the GS the app frame is 46.3 ms and its float layer is
     460,861 single-precision calls and ZERO doubles, so the layer that was
     34% of a CPU-raster frame is essentially all of this one.  The ceiling is
     therefore no longer a share to be argued but a subtraction: keep the
     instruction, drop the call.  Rows 7 and 8 (16.4) are that subtraction,
     and the 28-cycle price of 6.5 -- 15 cycles of which are the jal and its
     prologue -- is what makes it worth doing.
  2. THE STATIC BACKGROUND IS THE LARGEST CPU-SIDE PRIZE, AND ONLY FEWER
     PIXELS COUNT.  The copied-pixel row says a cached blit of the baked
     gradient saves 7 cycles a pixel and costs 1.5 MB, so it cannot be a
     cheaper REPRESENTATION -- it can only be a smaller AREA.  Everything that
     moves in a steady XMB frame is inside one full-width horizontal band,
     measured as 28.7% of the canvas.  Three facts about the mechanism: tinygl
     has no glScissor and glViewport is the only rect that clips a triangle;
     glClear is whole-plane and the only fill cheap enough to replace it is an
     opaque quad at 2.6 cycles; and the surface DOES persist across frames, so
     not painting outside the band leaves the right pixels there rather than
     garbage.
  3. THE SPAN LOOP IS NOT A LEVER and the mesh dial is not one either.  f_bg's
     row proves a scanline step is noise, so "short spans are what a fine mesh
     pays for" is false.  And the dial has been priced and lost: 32x32 ->
     16x16 is worth 47.0 ms and costs 10.81% of the sheet's pixels with a
     worst channel delta of 145/255 -- three orders of magnitude more than the
     affine trade below -- so it does not ship, and
     verify/tests/test_xmb_render.c refuses n < 32*32 as a law.
  4. A THIRD OF THE FILL IS PER-TRIANGLE AND LIBRARY-INTERNAL: ~27 ms of a
     173.8 ms frame in ztriangle.h's setup block rather than its span loop,
     two thirds of it integer.  This is bit-risky ground -- its answers come
     from (GLint)(fdy2*d1 - fdy1*d2) after fz has been DIVIDED through, so an
     integer rewrite would round in a different place -- and is the line to
     attack only with the twelve-PPM byte-identity gate in hand.

  Four cuts landed on this layer, each with its pixel price stated rather than
  implied:

  - Affine instead of perspective for a flat z (78.3 -> 41.7 ms of f_menu
    fill): 0.02% of the frame's pixels change, each a one-texel column on one
    vertical edge of a glyph; worst channel delta 102/255.  The px/span column
    is the row that says which other layers benefit the same way.
  - The (GLint) rewrite: float -> int has NO EE instruction (no trunc.w.s), so
    it ran the bit engine through a format descriptor and 32-bit halves.
    ps2_sf_fixsfsi() now reads the exponent band and emits 32 straight-line
    integer instructions with no call: 47 cycles instead of 536.  A conversion
    whose result cannot differ cannot change a pixel, so this saving has no
    visual price to watch.
  - One double that had no business being there: gl_clipcode()'s guard band
    was written `w1 * (1.0 + CLIP_EPSILON)` -- a double literal, so the
    GLfloat was promoted, the multiply ran on the bit engine, and the result
    was truncated back: three double calls and one float multiply per vertex,
    for a band whose whole purpose is to be off by one part in 100,000.
    Writing it `(GLfloat)(1.0 + CLIP_EPSILON)` took 10,713 double calls per
    frame to zero.  On a target where GLfloat IS double -- the hosted and Pi
    400 builds -- the cast is a no-op and the expression is bit-for-bit what
    it was.
  - The second blend recognition (TGL_BLEND_BRIGHTEN for GL_DST_COLOR/GL_ONE):
    f_ribbon's fill 61,328 -> 49,778 us and the steady frame 185,559 ->
    173,750 us, while f_bg, f_clear, f_surface and the ribbon's own geom
    column did not move at all -- 34 cycles per blended pixel, which is
    exactly the difference between the two prices above, and the second
    measured proof that in this rasteriser the DISPATCH, not the arithmetic,
    is what a blended fragment pays for.  Nothing about the picture changed,
    and that was shown three ways: a sweep over every sum the expression can
    produce (33,488,895 values, 0 mismatches), every (channel, multiplier)
    pair (1,051,392, 0 mismatches), and the twelve PPMs byte-identical with
    the recognition on and off -- shown to DISCRIMINATE by adding 1/255 of red
    inside the fast arm, which moves exactly the three frames the ribbon
    appears in and none of the other nine.

  One thing no measurement in this tree can settle: every number above is
  PCSX2's.  The identical image over SIO on a real console is the outstanding
  check.

11. The frame, attributed stage by stage
----------------------------------------

  LAW 11 (an app's frame is not the render its own log times).  The unit of
  presentation on this port is the PERIOD, and it is attributed by the cycle
  row, not by the app's draw column.  xb_paint() reports [XMBT] frame ...
  draw=<us> and stops at egl_swap_buffers(), while the pixels reach the GS
  only after the compositor has blitted the window's surface onto the canvas
  and the port has uploaded that rectangle.  The two halves are joined by
  [XMBT] cycle, which ps2_gui_pass() fills and ps2_task_dly() prints once the
  hold has been spent -- because the hold is part of the period the body sees,
  and nothing else times it.

    re-running its callback write the same pixels.  The walk used to read the
    composite's MODE instead of that flag, so one app's inval_wnd() billed

    shared change, and the selective-composite block of

    exactly one -- with the gate removed it reports "A rendered 1, B rendered
    1" and FAILS, while the pixel comparison still passes.  The tally is the
    assertion; the pixel diff is the safety net.

    filled none of the comp columns, so its cost appeared only as residue.  It

    safety net.  bfn=0 on a full=1 row is the premise corrected: the

11.4  A stall guard smaller than a real frame fires on every frame

  LAW 11b (an animation clock is stepped by elapsed time, never by frame
  count).  A clamp that replaces "impossible" with "nominal" must be larger
  than the worst REAL frame, and an effect clock must advance with time.

  WHY  Two defects stacked made "the 3D surface is not moving".  First,
       xb_paint had `if (dt < 0 || dt > 250) dt = 16`, and this machine's
       achieved cadence is 337-398 ms between paints -- so the guard replaced
       the true elapsed time with a nominal 16 ms EVERY FRAME, and everything
       that consumes dt ran at 1/21 of real time; a 167 ms menu tween took ten
       frames, i.e. 3.5 s.  The clock itself was never the problem: get_tim()
       is ps2_task_ms(), which accumulates real CP0 Count microseconds and
       reported 354/398/337 ms correctly while the app threw the number away.
       The bound is now 4,000 ms, which is a stall and not a slow frame.
       Second, the effect clock advanced 0.01 per DRAWN FRAME (the original
       gfx_display.c:874 does exactly that), so the wave's speed WAS the frame
       rate: at 2.7 frames a second the same 0.01 is 0.13 px/s, a surface that
       reads as frozen.  It is now `s_effect_time += dt / 1600.0f`, which is
       the same sequence of values at the designed cadence and real time at
       any other.

  MEASURE  the row that separates the three claims "not moving" could mean:

    [XMBT] wave n=2 dt=144 ms t1024=131 mid1024=334640 dm=495  dsum=-294
    [XMBT] wave n=6 dt=127 ms t1024=479 mid1024=335231 dm=-341 dsum=-875

  Before, the same row read dt=16 t1024=+10/frame dm=49..53 dsum=+-40.  After,
  the clock steps 217-246 in 1/1024ths per frame instead of 10, dm runs
  0.3-1.35 px with its sign flipping as the vertex crosses its extremum, and
  dsum -1262..-5091 against the +-40 the frozen sheet measured.

  GATE  the increment is bit-identical where the cadence is as designed:
        16.0f/1600.0f is the same float as 0.01f, and
        verify/tests/test_xmb_render.c drives exactly that (g_clock += 16 per
        paint), which is why 10 of its 12 PPMs are byte-identical across the
        change.  The two that differ are the two taken after the test's own
        deliberate 600 ms jump, where the new behaviour is the point.  Raising
        a clamp means trusting the unit of the clock underneath it, so the
        three cores that link src/apps/xmb.c were READ rather than assumed:
        core_arm64.c ticks at 1 kHz and says so, core_posix.c computes ms from
        gettimeofday, core_ps2.c derives ms from Count.

  What this does NOT buy is smoothness: an animation whose wall-clock duration
  is 167 ms cannot be shown in more than one frame by a machine that paints
  every 340 ms.  The menu now snaps instead of crawling, and the only way to
  get frames back for it is the pixel work above and the GS below.

11.5  A plate the stream buries is not a cost the EE pays

  LAW 11c (a refusal must be proven by set equality, never by a saving).
  frame= paints every window's flat body plate, and on a GS layer window the
  present uploads the band FIRST and sends the recorded stream after it -- so
  the stream's pixels land on top of that plate and the plate buys nothing.
  Refusing it is sound only while the refused set equals the rect the stream
  covers: a set equality, checked from both sides, and not a number that
  merely got smaller.

  MEASURE  the landing run, three cycles paired by cycle index; un-counted
           bench build, PS2_GL=eegs, read 2026-10-13:

  why the refusal stops at the client rect and no wider, and why the union of

  groups that each cover half.

PART FOUR -- THE INTERCONNECT: INPUT AS A REAL PLANE
=====================================================================

  ASYNC.txt 5 forbids "USB progress only in the GUI loop".  On this machine
  that prohibition became a 1 kHz plane with a measured 1.68 us handler, and
  the pointer became a chain of five layers whose units differ.  This Part
  states both, because every pointer symptom in this port's history came from
  measuring one layer's output and correcting it at another.

12. The 1 kHz plane and the HID handover
----------------------------------------

  LAW 12 (the plane collects; the pass delivers).  The interrupt handler reads
  the bus and NOTHING ELSE.  Everything with a side effect on the rest of the
  image -- the event-queue syscalls, the prints, the report processors --
  stays in the pass.

  WHY  A report read at present time inherits the GUI loop's latency, which is
       ui=16667 us plus whatever a frame costs.  And a handler that renders,
       allocates or blocks makes its own measured WCET meaningless, which is
       the only number that justifies running at 1 kHz at all.

  MEASURE  what crosses the boundary.  src/drivers/ps2/ps2_usb.c divides its
           own work in two: intr_take() is the bus half -- read the
           descriptor's condition code, eight uncached byte reads, advance the
           ring index, ed_unhalt(), intr_top_up() -- and is the only part that
           runs on the plane.  The handler therefore touches no heap, renders
           nothing, and calls no BIOS service that could block.

  LAW 12b (one queue, one writer per index).  hoff_push() publishes into a
  256-slot ring; the plane owns s_hoff_in and the pass owns s_hoff_out, and no
  other party writes either, so there is NO LOCK and no test-and-set in the
  handler.  A full queue increments dropped rather than overwriting an unread
  report.  hoff_drain() stamps each slot with the Count reading taken at the
  handler entry that collected it, so the hold the pass reports is the hold
  from the BUS, not from the moment the slot was written.

  LAW 12c (the handover happens once, and only on evidence).
  ps2_usb_input_plane(1) is called in the [ASYNC] boot block when the plane's
  own verdict is LIVE (got + 1 >= owed over the boot window), BEFORE the
  divisor and wcet rows print, so the cost of the collection is inside the
  measurement of the handler that performs it.  It sets s_plane_collects
  *before* installing the hook: the flag is the protocol, and installing a
  producer the consumer does not know about would collect into a queue nothing
  drains.  On DEAD or STALLED the pass keeps polling the rings itself -- a
  queue nobody fills is a dead keyboard, so the fallback is not optional.  The
  check for a reclaimed IOP-RAM shadow block also stays on the pass side,
  because reading it is a bus access and the handler has a budget.

  GATE   proven on this machine: the handler's cost with the collection inside
         it is `isr wcet=498 ticks = 1.68 us`, against `wcet=104 ticks = 0.35
         us` with an empty hook -- so a period of polling four interrupt rings
         costs about 1.3 us, 3.4% of the 50 us budget, with overruns=0.
         ASYNC.txt 10's 2 ms pointer-update gap is the gate on the other side:
         plane_held_max_us is the longest hold the pass has measured and is
         judged against 2000.

  What a headless run can and cannot show.  scripts/ps2_smooth.sh moves no
  host cursor, so a run legitimately prints reports=0.  The instrument that
  proves the collector runs with no hand on the mouse is `collected on 2223
  periods`: plane_periods counts handler entries that passed the host guard
  and reached the collector, and a period needs only the clock while a report
  needs motion.  plane_handover_miss is judged at 0 for a LIVE plane and
  SKIPped for any other verdict; plane_reports_in_pass must not grow once the
  plane owns the rings.  Non-zero plane_reports_collected and a non-zero
  plane_held_max_us require `make run-ps2` and a hand on the mouse:

    [PSTAT] plane: reports=0 on the input plane, 0 in a pass, dropped=0
                queue max=0 of 256, collected on 2223 periods
    [PSTAT] plane: held max=0 us mean=0 us over 0 delivered -- isr 1.68 us of the
                50 us budget, overruns=0

  One fact about the handover is easy to lose, because it is not a timing
  problem: the keyboard half of it is EDGE-ONLY.  A held key crosses this
  boundary as a press and, much later, a release -- nothing in between -- so
  the repeat a person expects is not missing from the bus, it was never on it.
  Who produces it, and on whose numbers, is chapter 19's LAW 19f.

13. Which layer owns the pointer
--------------------------------

  LAW 13 (one constant per layer).  A count means something different at each
  stage of this chain, and each stage has its own setting.  Correcting a
  layer's output at a different layer is how this pointer was tuned in circles
  for a session; the table in 13.4 is the thing the instruments read against.

13.1  The instruments: four commands, four different questions

  - ptrcal    -- IS THE MAPPING CORRECT?  It feeds synthetic reports through
    the driver's own report entry, so a count travels the same road as a count
    off the bus (decoded, scaled, bordered, posted as an event) with the hand
    out of the loop.  It turns the cap off for the run and restores it
    afterwards, because the cap is a policy about how fast a PERSON may be
    moved while ptrcal measures how a count becomes a pixel; left in place it
    would report the limiter as a broken mapping.  Its header prints the live
    source= and curve=, because under the hw profile the gain rows are MEANT
    to be non-linear -- that is the accelerator, not a fault -- and a [CAL]
    log read without its header says the opposite.
  - ptrstat   -- WHAT IS ACTUALLY ON THE WIRE?  It counts EVERY report, not
    the one-in-sixty-four the [RAW] rows print, and sorts them by magnitude.
    It closes the window since its last call, so the first call of a session
    reports whatever accumulated during boot: type `ptrstat boot` to discard
    that window, then perform a slide, then label the window that contains it.
  - ptgain    -- WHAT IS A COUNT WORTH TO THE HAND THAT SENT IT?  The only
    instrument here whose denominator is a human movement of known length
    rather than a constant, and therefore the only one that can CHECK
    PS2_EMU_POINTER_SCALE instead of assuming it.
  - ptrsrc    -- WHICH OF THE TWO PROFILES IS ANSWERING, AND WHY?  See 13.2.
  - sens / maxstep -- this port's own POLICY, applied only once the first two
    agree the path is sound.

  ptrcal drives the same report handler ptrstat counts, so it must not run
  inside a measurement window.  Every ptrstat call resets, so forgetting costs
  one keystroke.

  The decisive protocol is five windows:

    Window           Hand                        Decides
    ---------------------------------------------------------------
    ptrstat boot     nothing                       clears the warm-up
    ptrstat slow     one deliberate slide          distance at LOW speed
    ptrstat fast     THE SAME PHYSICAL SLIDE       ...and at HIGH speed
    ptrstat h        sideways only                 does the other axis stay 0
    ptrstat w        Mac cursor, left edge to      gain against a host distance
                    right edge                     that needs no measuring

  Read the `vs previous window` row:

    x = 1.0x          the wire carries DISTANCE.  A single guest gain is then
                      correct and sufficient, and sens is where the feel gets
                      set.
    x >> 1.0x         the numbers are already cursor pixels, multiplied by
                      speed by something OUTSIDE this port.  No constant can
                      be right for both rows.  The answer is NOT a cap:
                      dp_ptr_limit() spends a bounded distance per INPUT
                      period and abandons debt past DP_PTR_LIMIT_DEBT steps,
                      which makes where the cursor lands a function of hand
                      SPEED rather than hand DISTANCE -- the same
                      non-proportionality, moved.  What this port does instead
                      is take the ratio as evidence that the wrong source
                      profile is live; the gain belongs to the layer above
                      (macOS's own cursor curve, the only multiplier here that
                      acts on distance).
    still large       a real mouse that is not moving answers IN tokens with
                      NAKs, not with 00 00 00 reports, so a big still count
                      says a synthetic source is producing reports whether or
                      not anything moved.  Cross-check the e0/e1 column of the
                      [SCH] row.
    x max << 127      a genuine device saturates at |127| on a fast slide; a
                      source whose maximum never approaches it across a slide
                      as fast as a hand can manage is coalescing motion into
                      something other than device counts.
    reports -> N/s    the bus's effective polling rate, set by the device's
                      bInterval, NOT by this controller's 1 ms frame.  USB 1.1
                      gives low-speed interrupt pipes a 10 ms floor and
                      full-speed HID 1-8 ms, so 100-125/s is what a REAL PS2
                      mouse also reads at and is not by itself evidence of an
                      emulator.
    net << total      counts are leaking into the axis nobody asked for -- a leak no
      (the h window)   per-axis synthetic test can see, because the synthetic
                      sweep never moves both axes the way a hand does.
    border / onwall   what the screen edge did to the same window, in guest
                      pixels and per paint pass.  This is the row that answers
                      "why is the cursor sticky at the edges" and no count row
                      can, because the answer is about POSITION rather than
                      the wire.  A relative pointer's position is the integral
                      of its counts, so pixels the border discards are gone
                      for good while pushing further into the wall is free and
                      produces nothing: AN EDGE IS A ONE-WAY SINK, and leaving
                      it costs exactly as much host travel as entering it gave
                      away.  Read discarded as the share of the hand's
                      distance the wall ate: near zero means the cursor merely
                      RESTS at the edge and the stickiness is elsewhere; a
                      third or more means the gain is spending the whole 800
                      px canvas faster than the hand can cross the desktop it
                      sits on -- a saturation limit, lowered with sens, not
                      raised.

13.2  Two source profiles, chosen from the device's own identity

  A count off this bus is worth a different number of pixels depending on what
  produced it, and no single guest constant can be right for both cases.  The
  port therefore ships two stubs, picked from the enumerated device's own USB
  identity at boot rather than from where the code happened to be built:

    Profile  Selected when                       Gain        Curve       Cap
    ------------------------------------------------------------------------
    emu      the pointer answers as PCSX2's      96/256 =    none        off
             HID Mouse, device 0627:0001         0.375 guest
             (desc_id == 0x00010627)             px per count
    hw       anything else answers -- a mouse    verbatim    dp_ptr_     off
             somebody sells                      256         riscos() at
                                                           g_mouse_step_mult,
                                                           the Pi 400's curve

  Detection runs once at boot after the host engine has enumerated, and again
  after `usb probe`; the boot log says which one it picked, in both units:

    [PS2] Pointer source emu (detected): 96/256 px per count = 0.37 px per host
        cursor px, no curve, cap off

  `ptrsrc hw` / `ptrsrc emu` override that for a session, which is the only
  way to run the emulator's stub against a hardware-shaped count stream or the
  reverse without changing what is plugged in; `ptrsrc auto` hands the
  decision back to detection.  Switching profiles clears the carried subpixel
  remainder and any distance the previous one had not spent, so a change
  mid-session cannot leave the old source's half-pixel tail to be paid out by
  the new one.

  LAW 13b (one gain constant).  There is ONE gain in this stack:
  PS2_EMU_MULT_FP, reached from the console as `sens`.  PointerXScale and
  PointerYScale look like a second copy of it -- both are in the repo's ini,
  set to 8 -- but neither name exists anywhere in PCSX2 v2.8.2's source, a USB
  Type::Pointer binding has no scale factor at all (InputManager.cpp:1043-57),
  and its callback is handed the raw delta (:1398-1403).  Editing them changes
  nothing.  The same is true of PointerXSpeed, PointerYSpeed, PointerInertia
  and the two dead-zone keys: they are read (:1645-1653) and applied
  (:1372-81) to the SCALED value on the ProcessEvent path -- pad and lightgun
  bindings -- while the hidmouse callback gets the unscaled delta.  They are
  INERT here, not neutral, and the only live pointer setting in the ini is
  `[USB1] hidmouse_Pointer = Pointer-0`.

  Where 96 came from is a hand, not arithmetic: at 32/256 a stroke could not
  cross the 800 px canvas, at 256/256 the cursor sat pinned against a wall,
  and 96/256 is what has shipped between those two reports.  Those
  observations were taken against COUNTS, so they survive the scale correction
  unchanged; what does not survive is reading them as "3 px per host px",
  which was eight times too flattering -- the shipped pointer has been
  tracking the hand at a third of its travel all along.

    sens   px per count    guest px per host cursor px
    ---------------------------------------------------
      25      64/256                     0.25
      38      97/256        0.38 -- the shipped default
      51     130/256                     0.51
      64     163/256                     0.64
      77     197/256                     0.77
      89     227/256                     0.89
     100     256/256        1.00 -- the too-fast bound, counts verbatim

  Roughly ONE sens per hundredth of a guest px per host px.  The prompt value
  does not survive a reboot, so what a dial settles belongs in PS2_EMU_MULT_FP
  afterwards.

13.3  The cursor's frame rate is the paint pass's frame rate

  LAW 13c.  A relative pointer's screen position is only ever updated when the
  screen is repainted, so no gain, scale or accelerator changes the cursor's
  cadence; only the cost of a present does.

  WHY / MEASURE  the table that reversed this document's own conclusion.  The
                 old argument was that a narrow region "has to be uploaded a
                 row at a time" and therefore that bands must be FULL-WIDTH to
                 be cheap.  The per-row GIF setup is real -- 16 rows cost 10
                 us of upload, 32 rows 20 us, about 0.6 us a row -- and it is
                 nothing next to the 528 us the same 16x16 region costs to
                 render:

    region presented            px      us/pass   render    swap   upload
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    whole canvas 800x600      480,000     265,782  215,049  49,094   1,637
    one 16-row band, full w    12,800       5,370    4,015   1,309      45
    64-row band, full width     51,200      20,378   14,964   5,237     175
    cursor sprite 16x16             256        528      486      29      10
    80x16                          1,280      1,033      883     133      14
    16x80                          1,280      1,204    1,006     146      50

  Render and swap scale with PIXELS, not rows.  So the present is clipped in
  BOTH dimensions and a cursor pass presents the union of the old and new
  sprite boxes at 536-1050 us instead of 5,370.  Against the emulator's ~60
  reports a second, that is the difference between 27 painted positions per
  second (one screen update per four hand movements) and 137-267 -- which is
  why the supply is now the limiter rather than the repaint.

  The ceiling that bites is CADENCE, not width.  New motion is published once
  per emulated EE frame (Counters.cpp:500 VSyncStart -> PollSources), so at 60
  fps the host makes at most ~60 distinct reports a second however often OHCI
  polls at its 1 ms frame; button-unchanged motion coalesces into one slot of
  a 16-slot queue (hid.cpp:404-18), and a full queue drops (:383-89).  The
  |127| byte ceiling is NOT one of them: `dx = int_clamp(xdx, -127, 127); xdx
  -= dx;` carries a report's overflow into the next one, so no distance is
  lost at speed -- which is why ptrstat's sat= counts a saturated report, not
  a missing stroke.  And the emulator keeps a delta in a 16.16 accumulator,
  exchanges it whole at each poll (InputManager.cpp:1434, :1367) and truncates
  the float on the way into the device (usb-hid.cpp:731), so SUB-count
  fractions are dropped at that boundary.  At one count per host px, sat=
  cannot bite until 127 px of cursor travel inside one event, and a brisk drag
  reaches that only as a flick.  One constant, one boundary.

13.4  Which layer owns what, with the label each claim carries

  Read each block as one row of the pointer's topology: what the layer does to
  a movement, what can be set about it, and what the claim is worth.

  macOS
       behaviour  pointer acceleration and inertia, applied to the host cursor
                  before any byte exists
       setting    defaults write -g com.apple.mouse.scaling -1
       status     INFERRED (no raw mode in PCSX2 to bypass)

  PCSX2 hidmouse
       behaviour  one HID event per IN token; byte 0 = 3 buttons + pad, then
                  X, Y, wheel as three 8-bit signed fields; reports the MAC
                  CURSOR'S OWN DELTAS, not device counts
       setting    nothing -- the two ini Scale keys are read by no code
       status     PROVEN from v2.8.2 source: raw delta to the callback, no
                  scale on a Pointer binding, int_clamp carries the remainder,
                  motion once per EE frame -> ~60 reports/s

  OHCI frame
       behaviour  HcFmInterval = 0x27782EDF -> 11999 bit times at 12 Mbit/s =
                  1 ms, and the periodic list is walked once per frame, so a
                  device polls at 1/2/4/8/16/32 ms
       setting    bInterval at enumerate time
       status     PROVEN: the same word real PS2 firmware writes

  Real PS2 hardware
       behaviour  one OHCI shared with the IOP at IOP physical 0x1F801600,
                  Full Speed + Low Speed only, IOP interrupt 22, DMA
                  structures confined to the IOP's 2 MB; the host stack is an
                  IOP module and the EE is a client of it over SIF
       setting    nothing in this port
       status     spec-derived; our probe reading that base proves only that
                  PCSX2 decodes it, and "the EE cannot poll it on retail
                  hardware" is the open risk

  This port
       behaviour  source profile -> dp_ptr_scale()'s 8.8 gain (sens) ->
                  dp_ptr_limit() px per INPUT period (maxstep, off; the period
                  is the plane table's, scaled by the periods a pass actually
                  owes) -> the screen borders
       setting    ptrsrc, sens, maxstep
       status     PROVEN by ptrcal

  Two rows cut opposite ways.  The frame interval is a MATCH with real
  hardware -- the value our boot log measures is the value Sony's own usbd
  writes -- so the bus row will not change on a retail console and there is no
  point tuning for it.  The device-rate row is the opposite: 100-125 reports/s
  is normal there too, so the emulator cannot be blamed for it, and the only
  real-hardware divergence that matters for pointer FEEL is the macOS row
  above the wire.

13.5  Settled facts, so they are not re-opened

  - The emulator's |127| clamp is not biting (2026-09-24 run, 460 reports: max
    count on any axis 27, median 2, byte ceiling 127).  Lowering PointerXScale
    to buy headroom buys nothing; do not re-open this without a sat= column
    that is non-zero.
  - Nothing is dropped between the bus and the handler: [DEV] p= retired
    reports and the [RAW] sequence numbers agree (460 vs #448 at 1-in-64
    sampling) and [RING] n=1 max=1 says the ring never queued up.  The 20-50
    reports/s is what PCSX2 PRODUCES, not what this port loses.
  - The counts are small enough that the guest's own step is 3 counts: at
    96/256 a 1-count report pays out 0 px and holds the remainder in the
    carry, so with a median of 2 the pointer moves in whole pixels only every
    couple of reports.  Distance is not lost -- the carry keeps it -- but that
    is why slow motion reads as reluctance.
  - PS2_EMU_POINTER_SCALE is 1, settled from the emulator's source rather than
    inferred: across 460 reports the largest count was 27, and eight counts
    per host pixel would make that a cursor crawling under 10 px/s, which no
    hand does.  What ptgain still measures is what the source cannot say: what
    one count is worth on this Mac, whose own acceleration curve sits in front
    of every delta PCSX2 reads off QCursor::pos().  The shipped gain stayed at
    96/256 rather than moving to the 768/256 this row once prescribed, because
    the hand's two bounds were taken against counts -- keeping them
    means keeping 96.
  - ptgain's protocol: `ptgain 0`; park the MAC cursor hard against the left
    edge of the Mac's display and slide it in one unbroken stroke to the right
    edge -- the travel is exactly the display's logical width, which System
    Settings states, and it is the one host distance that needs no measuring;
    `ptgain <width>` reads the measured counts-per-host-px next to the
    constant it checks; `ptgain <width> <percent>` sets the gain so the cursor
    moves percent/100 guest px per host px.  It warns when the y totals say
    the sweep was not straight, and caps the gain at 1024/256 because
    dp_ptr_scale() truncates a report at DP_PTR_MAX_PIXELS, so above that the
    top of a fast stroke would be eaten inside the shaper instead of here
    where the row says so.  Its `loop:` column is the pass rate -- the
    pointer's frame rate -- and a low number there is steppiness that no gain
    constant will smooth.
  - [PSTAT] lat: stamps the report that opens an empty queue and reads after
    the flush, so it is queueing PLUS the paint -- the latency the eye
    actually gets, not the one loop: only implies.  A large average with a
    small maximum is a uniformly slow repaint; a large maximum on a small
    average is one stall somewhere else in the loop.
  - Read the RATIO between the paint columns, not the milliseconds: PCSX2 does
    not model EE cycle counts, so absolute timings are the emulator's, while
    the proportion between render, swap and upload is instruction counts --
    and that is what picks the fix.

=====================================================================
PART FIVE -- THE PRESENTATION PLANE: THE EE/GS BACKEND
=====================================================================

  Part Three priced the pixel and Part Four proved that the EE pays that price
  inside its own deadline.  This Part states the consequence: the per-fragment
  work moves to the one programmable rasteriser the console has, and the laws
  that make that arrangement legal on this machine are stated before the code
  that follows them.

14. Why the Graphics Synthesizer, and only it
---------------------------------------------

  LAW 14 (the presentation plane belongs to the engine that owns the pixels).
  Per-fragment work shall run on the Graphics Synthesizer.  The Emotion Engine
  shall keep per-vertex work, state, and the bus.

  WHY  It is not an optimization preference, it is arithmetic from chapter 10.
       The frame the user needs is 16,700 us.  One of the XMB's own draw
       functions, f_bg, costs 29.4 ms of touching pixels -- 1.75x the whole
       budget by itself -- and the entire float layer's measured ceiling,
       every call in it replaced by the fastest software path this port has,
       is 1.51x.  No amount of work on the EE closes a 31x overrun through a
       1.51x door.  The GS is the only hardware rasteriser on this machine, so
       the answer to a per-fragment cost is to stop paying it on the EE.

  MEASURE  chapter 10's price list and chapter 11's attribution, read on the
           same frame: the fill is where the milliseconds are, and under
           PS2_GL=eegs the XMB's draw= goes 176.5 ms -> 74.047 ms with the
           identical sources.

  GATE  the present host suite, 64 checks, and the image's rows 50-56; 5c's
        own result is the user's eyes, because it is the first increment that
        can change what a window looks like (see 22 for why that is a gate and
        not a confession).

  Four facts fix the shape before any code is written, and all four are read
  from this tree rather than from a manual, which is what makes them binding:

  FACT 1  The surface an app renders into is the compositor's blit SOURCE.
          egl_create_window_surface() sets surf->pixels = wnd->dev->pixels
          (src/gl/egl_surface.c:40) and redraw_all_windows_clip() blits
          src = wnd->dev->pixels into g_screen_dev->pixels row by row
          (src/window/wnd.c:539).  The GS cannot write EE memory -- its
          primitives land in its own local VRAM -- so a GS backend cannot keep
          that arrangement.  The GL window's contents stop being an array the
          CPU copies and become something the GS itself composes.  This is the
          one architectural consequence of the change; every law in 15 follows
          from it.

  FACT 2  What the apps actually ask for is a 2D UI, which the GS does
          natively.  xmb.c uses 18 of gl_ops_t's 38 entry points: 2D vertices,
          affine texture coordinates (glTexCoord2f, never a q), per-vertex
          RGBA, GL_QUADS, glBlendFunc, one bound texture per quad, and no
          depth at all.  Gouraud shading is PRIM.IIP, affine texture
          interpolation is TEX0/TEX1 with MMIN/MMAG, and blending against the
          destination already in VRAM is ALPHA with BLEND_COLOR_DEST under
          PRIM.ABE -- all hardware.  glgears.c is the counter-example and
          stays on TinyGL: it needs lighting and display lists, for which the
          GS has no hardware.

  FACT 3  The GS's coordinate window is 12.4 fixed-point around a 2048 offset.
          Vertices are integer-quantised at 1/16 px, which is invisible for a
          768x500 UI, and it is the reason a first increment needs no VU
          program: an EE-side float-to-packet conversion per vertex is enough
          when the vertex count is thousands and the pixel count is 384,000.
          Lane 12 is what a VU would buy once the vertex count matters.

  FACT 4  The port already owns the DMA path it needs.  ps2_gs.c builds
          GIFTAGs, primes BITBLTBUF/TRXPOS/TRXREG/TRXDIR and streams DMAC
          channel 2 today.  The primitive path is the same pipe with
          FLG=PACKED and a different REGS list, so nothing here touches the
          driver's blit path and the canvas upload stays as it is.

  LAW 14b (the reference is headers, never source).  This backend is written
  against third_party/ps2sdk/ as a description of hardware only --
  ee/draw/include/*.h, ee/libgs/include/libgs.h, common/include/gif_tags.h,
  common/include/gs_gp.h.  No .c from that tree is linked or copied, in this
  or any later increment.

  WHY  The port's cleanroom law and its single-source law are the same law: a
       borrowed library would be a second model of the GS's field tables, and
       it would drift exactly where drift is invisible on a screen -- one
       field's bit position, one rounding of a clip.

  Selection: a user setting AND a build flag, both naming the same three
  answers.

    OpenGL PS2 : [x] TinyGL   [ ] EE/GS

  following the existing radio-pair idiom (g_wm_style / pmc_set_style(), drawn
  in src/settings/appearance.c:114-115 and hit-tested at :160-171).  The value
  lives with the GL layer, not in the settings file, and in its own TU
  (src/gl/gl_backend_choice.c) because it has three callers on two sides of a
  link boundary -- gl_backend_select()/gl_backend_selected() are the applet's,
  gl_backend_default() is the build's answer, gl_backend_software_default() is
  the rule that used to be the EGL_DEFAULT_BACKEND macro in egl_surface.c (now
  deleted).  There is one of each, which is what stops the three drifting
  apart.

  LAW 14c (a choice nobody made reads as the build's answer).  So the default
  image is byte-for-byte the behaviour this section described before the row
  existed.  LAW 14d (the row is PS2-scoped, and provably so): the row is #if
  defined(BTRON_PS2_TARGET)-scoped, because src/settings/*.c is linked by
  every target including the Pi 400, which links no GL source at all and must
  keep identical behaviour -- verified: the row's three strings are in
  btron-ps2.elf and in zero bytes of btron-posix.  LAW 14e (a choice is
  remembered, not applied): egl_create_window_surface() asks
  gl_backend_selected() at every surface creation (egl_surface.c:38), so it
  takes effect at the NEXT GL window and not under the one on screen;
  gl_resize, egl_make_current and egl_destroy_surface keep branching on
  gl_active_backend() -- what is installed, never what was requested.

  The build dial is the Makefile's PS2_GL=, which had two values and now has
  three: tinygl (the shipped image), virgl (the other software rasteriser,
  kept for the timing comparisons [XMBT] already makes), and eegs, which makes
  the image BOOT on the backend the row offers.  That is the point of it: an
  A/B against the GS pipeline is `make ps2 PS2_GL=eegs`, not a walk to the
  Display applet -- and the applet still outranks the build on all three.
  Splitting the rule in two is what makes the third value expressible at all,
  because a placeholder that answers a GS request with "the default" would, on
  a build dialled to eegs, be a call to itself.

  LAW 14f (an unknown dial value is an error, not a silent tinygl).  WHY: for
  the reason every measurement law in this book gives -- a typo builds an
  image that works, prints timings, and describes a pipeline nobody asked for.
  GATE: the value itself is stamped into .build/ps2-gl.stamp and compared by
  content, with the objects that read it deleted when it disagrees (make
  cannot see a CHANGE of flag; the three objects stay newer than their sources
  and get linked into an image built for the other value).  And the gate must
  be handed the same value as the build -- `make test-ps2 PS2_GL=eegs` --
  because test-ps2 depends on the image and would otherwise rebuild it back to
  tinygl and grade a different artifact.  That is not a hypothetical: it is
  how the first attempt at row 52's control passed while believing it was
  testing the eegs image.  verify/tests/test_gl_backend_choice.c is the other
  half of the same law, one compile per dial value, because an image row can
  only see the value the shipped image was built with, and a flag that
  selected NOTHING looks exactly like a flag that selected tinygl.

  Three rows read the selection law off the image (scripts/test_ps2.sh 50-52):
  gl_init calls tinygl_backend_init twice, once per arm; the same function
  contains no jal to gl_backend_software_default and none to
  gl_backend_default, which are the placeholder's shape and the recursion's;
  and the constant gl_backend_default returns is at most 3, since 3 is what
  PS2_GL=eegs means and the two rows before it are what make the NAME mean
  something rather than the number.  Each was shown to go red: restoring the
  pre-5c placeholder arm fails the first with "1 times"; (GL_BACKEND)7 as the
  default fails the bound with the two above holding.

15. The standing laws of the present path
-----------------------------------------

  5c is the increment that makes this book's Part Five true of the shipped
  image rather than of a plan.  Six laws, each earned by writing its
  consequence down before the code, and each stated here with the consequence
  attached -- because three of the six exist to prevent a failure that has no
  symptom on a console.

  LAW 15a (activation installs the linked pipeline and names itself).
  gl_init()'s GL_BACKEND_EEGS case takes g_gl = &g_tinygl_ops and sets the
  active backend to GL_BACKEND_EEGS: the GS stage hangs on TinyGL's own
  draw_triangle_* hooks, so the CPU rasteriser still builds vertices,
  clipping, textures and state, and only the span loops are replaced.
  CONSEQUENCE: every test that asked gl_active_backend() == GL_BACKEND_TINYGL
  was really asking "is the linked CPU front end live?", and once EEGS is
  active six of them are wrong -- egl_make_current() and egl_destroy_surface()
  would take the virgl arm of a build with no virgl context,
  gl_resize()/gl_shutdown() the same, and gl_fill_stats() would answer a
  caller's question with zeroes.  They now ask one predicate,
  gl_tinygl_pipeline(), true for TINYGL AND EEGS.  A `|| EEGS` written six
  times is six places to forget the seventh.

  LAW 15b (the order on the bus is canvas, environment, primitives -- so the
  stream is staged).  The recorder hands each full group to its sink while the
  application is still drawing, and the composite that drives the drawing runs
  BEFORE ps2_gs_upload().  A sink that went straight to DMAC channel 2 would
  put every triangle into VRAM and then overwrite it with the canvas's stale
  copy of the same rectangle -- stale, because LAW 15c has just stopped the
  blit.  So src/gl/eegs_present.c owns a per-frame staging buffer; the sink
  appends whole groups to it; the environment packet and the staged groups go
  out after the uploads.  The DMA stays one send per group, because a group is
  already a whole GIF packet with its own EOP and NLOOP -- staging changes
  WHEN a group is sent, never HOW.

  GATE  row 55, the bus order as three call-site addresses inside one
        function, whose control is the regression itself: moving the band's
        ps2_gs_upload() below the present block fails 55 ALONE, with 53 and 54
        holding.  That is the evidence row 55 measures an order and not a
        presence.

  CAPACITY, measured rather than modelled: the estimate was about 13,700
  quadwords for an XMB frame; the frame turned out to be 29,787, and the
  counter added for exactly this said so on the first boot -- `drop=42`, the
  stage holding the first nine groups in recorded order and refusing the other
  forty-two, which is "what would have reached the screen was the beginning of
  a frame." EEGS_STAGE_QW_CAP is 65,536 now (1 MiB of .bss, and .bss costs no
  image bytes against chapter 4's budget).  The suite's overflow row counts
  its triangles from the cap rather than from a constant, so raising the cap
  again cannot quietly turn that row into a pass that tests nothing.

  LAW 15c (one layer, and it is a property of the window).  EGL_SURFACE is the
  only object that knows both the WND and which backend answered gl_init(), so
  egl_create_window_surface() marks the window gl_layer when the GS is live.
  redraw_all_windows_clip() then does two different things for that window: it
  still calls its paint -- that is what records the stream -- and it no longer
  blits the client pixmap into the canvas.  The second half has a consequence
  the first does not: the canvas stops holding a picture of the layer AT ALL,
  so any band that presents over the layer's rect must have the layer
  re-recorded underneath it.  Hence the rule in two halves --
  wnd_damage_needs_paint() answers TRUE when a layer window intersects the
  damage (otherwise a banded pass picks the blit_only composite and the layer
  is never re-recorded), and the walk re-runs the layer window's paint even
  when its img_valid is TRUE.  GATE: row 54, both of the layer's facts come
  from the window manager rather than a copy of them (wnd_gl_layer_take,
  wnd_gl_cover_take).

  LAW 15d (what is drawn over the layer is re-uploaded after it, one rect at a
  time, and only by a producer that paints its whole rect opaquely).  The
  panel and its gold bar, the bottom test bars, an open deskbar or tracker
  dropdown and the IME candidate window are composited into the canvas and
  uploaded with the band, which the primitive stream now follows -- so
  wherever the layer's rect overlaps them, the GS buries them.  The compositor
  therefore registers each canvas region it writes after the window walk
  (wnd_gl_cover_add()) and the present re-uploads every registered rect
  intersected with the layer, after the stream.  Two consequences fell out of
  writing that down.  The accumulator cannot be a UNION: the panel is rows
  0-28 and the bars are the last 40, so their union is the whole canvas, and
  re-uploading a union would replace the entire layer with the canvas's stale
  copy of it.  It is a bounded list -- eight entries, with coverdrop= in
  [GSTS] because a dropped rect is a buried overlay with no evidence attached
  -- and the cost is one small BITBLT per producer per frame (the panel is
  5,600 quadwords against a whole-canvas upload's 120,000).  That is also why
  the present does not upload the complement of the layer: those pixels are
  already on the bus.  And a producer may register ONLY what it paints
  opaquely over its whole rect, because the canvas holds no picture of the
  layer to restore.  The pointer sprite does not qualify: it writes its own
  60-odd white and black pixels and leaves the rest of its 16x16 box as the
  canvas had it, so uploading that box over the layer would replace the
  layer's pixels with the desktop background from before it.  Hence the sprite
  is not a cover producer, and the pointer is invisible while it is inside a
  GS window -- the one visible thing 5c does not close, and not a thing the
  cover mechanism can close.  The cure is a primitive, not another canvas
  upload: ALPHA at source-over is (Cs - Cd) * As + Cd, which returns Cd
  untouched wherever the source alpha is 0 and paints Cs wherever it is 255,
  so a 16x16 CT32 plane holding the cursor bitmap and one textured quad at the
  pointer position draw the pointer THROUGH the layer, clipped to it by the
  same SCISSOR -- and outside it the canvas keeps drawing the pointer exactly
  as today.  That is increment 6.

  LAW 15e (the environment's four negotiation words have one answer each, and
  only ALPHA needed a fact).  FRAME names the page the PCRTC already reads --
  page 0, FBW 13, CT32, FMSK 0 -- because a primitive and a blit that disagree
  about where a pixel lives have no way to say so.  ZBUF sits at the last 8 KB
  page of VRAM, above every plane the cursor has handed out, with ZMSK 1 while
  TEST.ZTEN is 0: nothing reads it and nothing writes it, so its only duty is
  not to be someone else's address, and choosing it without the allocator
  leaves increments 3 and 5b's hand literals where they are.  TEST is 0 -- no
  alpha test, no depth test, every fragment passes.  ALPHA is
  gs_pack_alpha(BLEND_COLOR_SOURCE, BLEND_COLOR_DEST, BLEND_ALPHA_SOURCE,
  BLEND_COLOR_DEST, 0), the selects the GS's own equation color = (c1 - c2) *
  a >> 7 + c3 names, which is (Cs - Cd) * As + Cd -- what TinyGL's
  TGL_BLEND_ALPHA_ADD and the XMB's GL_SRC_ALPHA / GL_ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA ask,
  spelled in the hardware's terms.  The field codes are WHY ps2_gs_pack.h
  declined to name them at increment 2: position 3 is an ALPHA select where 0
  means As, while the other three are COLOUR selects where 0 means Cs, so the
  same numeral does not mean the same thing in the four places.  They are
  named in the header now, because a gate pins them -- and the control says
  what for: writing the destination select as Cs instead of Cd costs 2,
  [FAIL] ALPHA is (Cs - Cd) * As + Cd  got=0x...04 want=0x...44.
  CLAMP/TEX0/TEX1 go into the environment packet as zero, because it names no
  plane: eegs_texture() emits the trio as an AD group before any vertex that
  samples, and need_plane() will not let TME be set without one.

  LAW 15f (one GL target, process-wide).  g_gl is a single global
  (gl_dispatch.c:21) and TinyGL's ZBuffer is one static pair, so the image has
  one GL target, "last surface created wins", and no per-window backend choice
  exists to fall back on.  This is a limit of the port, not of the GS; it is
  why a second GL window opened while a layer exists draws into the FIRST
  window's rect, and why glgears stays on the CPU (fact 2).

  What 5c does not close, stated so nobody has to rediscover it: the pointer
  and any overlay that is not an opaque rectangle (increment 6); glClear(),
  which is TinyGL's own framebuffer fill and NOT a primitive, so the layer's
  background is the canvas's pixels showing through wherever a triangle does
  not land -- invisible for the XMB, whose mesh covers all 768x500 of its
  client area (box=0,0..767,499 is that census), and a real gap for an app
  that clears and draws less; LAW 15f's one drawing environment and one frame
  buffer; and D2_QWC, a 12-bit field, while the canvas upload has chunked at
  8,000 quadwords since before this plan existed -- a fact about every
  Host-to-Local transfer on real hardware, found here because 5c had to state
  the bound, and left alone because changing it moves every upload number in
  this book.

  LAW 16b (capacity is a latency knob, never a correctness limit).  A full
  recorder buffer is sent and a new group started rather than a vertex dropped
  -- legal because a packed group's NLOOP counts vertices and a GIF transfer
  may carry any number of groups.  The one exception is an op this recorder
  does not know, where the vertex is refused and unsplit= counts it.  So no
  application can overflow the recorder, and what the counters report is DMA
  starts per frame -- which is the number that costs milliseconds.  A stream
  that DROPS a tail is the failure mode designed out, because a partial frame
  with no evidence is exactly the bug that cannot be seen on a screen already
  600 ms behind; and 5c met that failure one layer downstream, in the staging
  buffer rather than the recorder, which is why the drop counter is in both.

  The measured shape of the law, on the shipped frame:

    verts      7,434   = 2,478 primitives x 3 corners, because the seam row 5
                        chose is handed one triangle at a time AFTER clipping
                        (clip.c:271-284) and records its three every time --
                        f_ribbon's 1,828 triangles are 5,484 recorded vertices
                        from its 1,984 mesh ones
    qw         29,853  = 7,434 x 4 + 51 group tags, +66 from the vertices a
                        continued strip re-emits
    groups        51   -- plus the one environment packet, so a frame costs 52
                        DMA starts, not the 4 the shipped 4,096-QW capacity
                        predicts.  The group's SIZE bound is not what closes a
                        group in practice; the STATE change is.
    gate         64 QW -- test-ps2-gs-rec overrides the capacity in its own
                        #define, where a group is 12 vertices in 49 and a frame
                        is 286 starts.  That is the knob, and the reason the gate
                        turns it rather than the code.

  The fourth quadword per vertex is the price of a claim this book does not
  make.  PRIM's texture, blend and fog flags are decremented by the hardware
  as each primitive completes, under PRMODE.MODE/OFFSET, so a strip that sets
  them once loses them from its second triangle on -- how multipass texturing
  is done, and the opposite of what a UI wants.  This recorder re-sends PRIM
  per vertex and claims nothing about PRMODE's layout.  The cheaper answer
  programs PRMODECONT and PRMODE (offsets 0x1A/0x1B, already named in
  ps2_gs_pack.h) into the environment stream once, with the decrement select
  at "no decrement", and drops PRIM from the REGS list: a third of the bus
  traffic, for a field table this book does not yet hold with enough
  confidence to gate.  Recorded as the follow-up it is -- when MODE[3:0],
  OFFSET[6:4], A_MODE[10:8] and CONTEXT[11] are confirmed and pinned by a hand
  literal like every other field here, GS_REC_REGS_N goes 4 -> 3 and the
  per-vertex price goes with it.  INFERRED, and labelled so by R2.

  Two laws make every row above readable, and they bind every increment:

  LAW 16c (a byte scan must be operand-general).  0d010046 is ONE pairing of
  trunc.w.s $f4,$f4; a C cast compiles to trunc.w.s $f6,$f6 = 0x4600318d.  The
  row that shipped as a literal grep was green on an image containing nine of
  the instructions it existed to forbid.  Match the opcode's fixed fields,
  never a whole instruction, and check the pattern against a real compiled
  instance before trusting it.

  LAW 16d (a positive control must be able to turn the row that names it red,
  and the harness must survive proving it).  A gate that cannot go red is not
  an instrument.  Every failure count in the record above is one edit, and the
  row it convicts is named beside it.

  Lanes.  Five things remain structurally open, and each is one of LAW 1's
  missing agents rather than a slow subroutine.  They are numbered the way
  increments are, so a console line and a lane can be cited in the same
  sentence.

  LANE 9  GS compositor token posting -- the presentation plane's producer.
    Close   VIOLATION 1's biggest single share: the 613,440 px blit and the
            480,000 px background restore, both of which are rectangle copies

    Budget  The lane has a floor under it, and the floor is a row, read

            which is what this Close cell has said all along.

    Close   VIOLATION 2, ps2_gs_gif_send()'s `while (D2_CHCR & (1 << 8)) ;`.

            stopped by its consumer, so the ring needs a bounded capacity, a
            drop count, and a completion check at the NEXT yield rather than
            a spin at this one.  drop= and coverdrop= already prove this

    Close   VIOLATION 3.  Per-task stacks and preemption at the boundary,

    Close   the per-vertex arithmetic that row 7 moved onto COP1 but still

  Everything above is about time.  This Part is about the parts: which unit

17. Drivers and files
---------------------

  LAW 17 (a driver is cleanroom, register-level, and imports no vendor code).
  No line of any SDK is linked or copied into this port
  (src/cores/core_ps2.c:13 states the rule at the top of the file); ps2sdk
  appears only as a description of hardware, per LAW 14b.  Every driver here
  was written against a register table and a manual, and every one of them is
  named in a gate row rather than trusted by its existence.

  The units, each in one line, with the law it carries:

    boot_ps2.s     EE reset vector, $gp and $sp, unrolled BSS wipe, the BIOS
                   syscall wrappers SetGsCrt and PutIMR, then ps2_kernel_main.
                   Its wait loop is the image's second .set mips3 exception
                   (5).
    ps2.ld         the layout: .text at 0x0100000-class addresses, and the
                   ASSERT that fails the LINK when _end reaches the stack or
                   the 32 MB RDRAM end -- chapter 4's budget as a link error.

    ps2_builtins.c the whole soft-float runtime the image links against:

  LAW 17b (a launcher row is reachable with the D-pad alone), which is three
  rules, each earned by a measured defect:

  1  Two key encodings are live and BOTH are accepted.  A windowed host
     forwards its own keysyms (SDLK_UP/DOWN are 0x111/0x112,
     src/window/event.c) while every bare-metal driver -- the pad, the PS2 USB
     keyboard, the Mac Quadra ports -- injects the BTRON_KEY_* codes from
     include/btron/event.h (RIGHT 0x4000004F, LEFT ...50, DOWN ...51, UP
     ...52).  A handler matching one is dead on the other half of the targets;
     tracker_handle_key() used to match only the keysym, which is why the
     D-pad moved the highlight by nothing but the pointer's new pixel.
  2  While a menu is open the D-pad must not displace the cursor, and Cross
     must become Return.  tracker_handle_mouse_move() recomputes hover_index
     from the pixel it lands on, the row pitch is TRACKER_ITEM_HEIGHT = 20 px
     and one D-pad step was 16 px, so a move that accompanies the arrow
     selects whichever row the pointer reached.  A click is worse: it is
     delivered at the pointer, not at the highlighted row.
  3  A menu handler consumes a key only when it ACTED on it.

18. The GS framebuffer architecture
-----------------------------------

  LAW 18 (one canvas, and it is the compositor's own array).  The GIF DMA
  reads the very memory the compositor painted.  core_ps2.c installs
  s_desktop_backbuffer into the driver with ps2_gs_set_framebuffer() as the
  FIRST act of ps2_kernel_main(), before the SIO and the GS come up, because
  the boot log is written into the canvas the screen shows.  There is no
  second framebuffer and no copy between two of them: the array the driver
  used to own (ps2_fb_memory, 800x600x4 = 1,920,000 bytes of .bss) and the
  one-word-per-pixel sweep that filled it (blit_backbuffer_rect_to_ps2fb,
  49,094 us of a full present) are both gone from the tree.

  WHY  Because the premise that made them necessary is false on this port: THE
       EE'S L1 CACHES ARE NEVER ENABLED.  Every coprocessor-0 write in the
       image is to Status (register 12 select 0) and there are exactly three
       of them -- btron_fp_init() raising CU1, ps2_ee_enable_interrupts() and
       ps2_ee_exit_handler() setting the global enable bit 16 (EIE) by
       read-modify-write, because PCSX2 does not decode ei (Unknown R5900
       COP0).  Nothing is written to the cache-control registers and no cache
       maintenance instruction exists anywhere, so the caches stay as the BIOS
       left them -- and the upload path reads its source through the uncached
       KSEG1 alias regardless (UNCACHED() in ps2_gs.c).  Every alias of an
       address reaches the same RDRAM and a bus master sees a store as soon as
       it lands.

  GATE  the single-canvas rows assert the premise and not the symptom: no
        ps2_fb_memory symbol, no present-path sweep, exactly one screen-sized
        CT32 object in the image and that it IS s_desktop_backbuffer,
        ps2_gs_set_framebuffer present as the install point, every mtc0 in the
        disassembly a Status write, none of them naming register 9 -- Count is
        the clock chapter 8 measures and writing it would break every elapsed
        time in the image -- and no cache op.

  LAW 18b (VRAM is addressed in two units, and both units are the whole of
  it).  The GS has 4 MB of eDRAM, laid out in pages of 2,048 32-bit words
  (8,192 B) and blocks of 64 words (256 B), with non-linear column-interleaved
  swizzling.  A texture or transfer address (TEX0.TBA, BITBLTBUF.SBA/DBA) is a
  14-bit index of 256-byte units and 16,384 x 256 = 4,194,304 exactly; a frame
  or Z-buffer base (FRAME.FBA, ZBUF.ZBA) is a 9-bit index of 8,192-byte units
  and 512 x 8,192 = 4,194,304 exactly.  Each field's width times its unit is
  the whole of VRAM, which is what makes the reading unambiguous rather than
  remembered -- and it falsified a comment in ps2_gs_pack.h that said 256-byte
  units for FRAME.FBA.  The encoders take a raw field value so no built word
  changed, but a caller's arithmetic would have been off by 32x.

  Consequence, arithmetic rather than taste (and the reason chapter 15's laws
  are shaped as they are): the canvas is CT32 at FBW 13 (832 px) x 600 =
  1,996,800 B; the XMB's GL window is 776x536 (xmb.c:3331 takes the desktop
  minus 24 and 64), whose own CT32 layer at FBW 13 would be 1,783,808 B; the
  icon atlas is a 384x384 upload but what the GS must address is the plane
  TinyGL allocated -- 512x512, because gl_image_alloc() rounds each axis up to
  a power of two and TEX0.TW/TH can only name one -- 1,048,576 B, not the
  589,824 B of staging pixels the app handed over.  Those three sum to
  4,829,184 B, which is 634,880 B MORE than the GS has, before the font sheet
  or any Z buffer.

  LAW 18c (no second full-screen surface).  The backend renders into the very
  canvas the CPU blit has just filled, with SCISSOR clipped to the window's
  rect.  What the allocator therefore reserves is the canvas at page 0
  (1,996,800 B rounded up to 244 pages of 8 KB), then texture pages out of the
  remaining 268: the icon plane 128, the font sheet's 128x128 plane 8, leaving
  132 pages -- 1,081,344 B -- for a wallpaper-as-texture, further atlases, and
  the one 8 KB page a Z buffer needs if a depth test is ever turned on.  A
  plane's span is a whole number of 16 KB units, not of 8 KB pages: a CT32
  picture is laid out in 4 KB blocks of 32x32 texels that pair into pages, and
  a plane's address enters fields of different units, so rounding to 16 KB is
  a whole number of blocks, of pages and of pages in even pairs.  The cost for
  the two planes this port has is nil, and it is the difference between a
  future 64-wide plane and its neighbour's texels arriving in the same block.

  LAW 18d (the canvas crosses as a blit, not as primitives).  B-System
  presents the desktop through the hardware Host-to-Local blitter rather than
  by rasterising the canvas: a full-screen primitive would re-introduce
  sub-pixel rounding, page-boundary clipping and per-packet DMA overhead for
  pixels that are already computed.  Five quadwords prime a transfer --

    BITBLTBUF (0x50)  DBA, DBW, DPSM = GS_PSM_CT32
    TRXPOS    (0x51)  DSAX, DSAY          (destination on the GS page)
    TRXREG    (0x52)  RRW, RRH            (the rectangle's extent)
    TRXDIR    (0x53)  0 = Host -> Local

  with the canvas's own DBW = (800+63)/64 = 13, and the pixels then stream as
  IMAGE-format packets (FLG=2) over DMAC channel 2, chunked at 8,000 quadwords
  because D2_QWC is a 12-bit field.  CT32 needs an even RRW and the stream is
  whole quadwords (4 px) wide, so a rect is widened to both, which stays
  inside the 832-pixel VRAM page.  A Host-to-Local IMAGE transfer carries no
  row pitch, so a narrow rectangle uploads one row at a time and a full-width
  band uploads one rectangle.  The source half of BITBLTBUF is left at zero
  knowingly: a Host-to-Local transfer ignores SBA/SBW/SPI because the source
  IS the quadword stream, so the destination half is the measured half.

  LAW 18e (the present is rect-limited, and a box rather than a row band).
  The rectangle comes from a set of up to PS2_BAND_MAX merged boxes --
  y-touching damage unions its x span, overflow falls back to the whole canvas
  -- and it is a box because the render pass costs about 0.3 us per pixel: 16
  rows at full width is 5,370 us with the byte-swap sweep still in the path,
  the same 16 rows clipped to the cursor sprite's own 16 columns is 528 us.
  Deleting the sweep is what the swap column measures; the gate expects it to
  read 0, and did: the whole-canvas row went from 274 ms to 265.782 ms and a
  16x16 sprite present costs 528 us.

  Video timing.  The shipped default is NOT the NTSC frame mode most PS2 prose
  assumes, and the reason is an emulator fact worth stating precisely:

    mode              SetGsCrt        SMODE2   DISPFB            DISPLAY
    ----------------  --------------  -------  ----------------  -------------------------
    800x600 on 720p   (0, 0x52, 0)    0x00     FBP 0, FBW 13,    DX 420 DY 40,
    (default)                          (prog)   PSM CT32          MAGH 0 MAGV 0,
                                                                  DW 799 DH 599
    DTV 480p          (0, 0x50, 0)    0x00     FBP 0, FBW 10,    DX 232 DY 35,
                                                                  MAGH 1, 1439x479
    NTSC 640x448      (1, 2, 1)       0x03     FBP 0, FBW 10,    DX 636 DY 50,
    frame mode        (interlaced)    (INT+    (CT32)             MAGH 3 MAGV 0,
                                                                  DW 2559 DH 447

    PMODE is 0xFF63 in all three: Circuit 1 + Circuit 2 enabled, SLBG = 0, so
    the frame buffer is the output.  BGCOLOR is (24, 48, 80), which is what
    fills the raster outside DW/DH.

  WHY the default is a 720p timing carrying an unscaled 800x600 window instead
  of a hardware-correct VESA 800x600@60 mode word: PCSX2 keys its entire CRT
  geometry off SMODE1's CMOD and LC and gives every VESA mode the one 640x480
  table entry (GSState.cpp VideoModeOffsets[2]), which GetResolution() then
  clamps the output to -- so a VESA timing can only ever show 640 of our 800
  columns and 480 of our 600 rows there, and its DY=25 is read as a -9 line
  offset because that entry expects baseDY=34.  The 720p entry is 1280x720
  with baseDY=24, so the same canvas comes out unclipped and unshifted.  On a
  real PS2 this paints 800x600 into the top-left of a 720p frame.  PROVEN on
  this emulator, by the monitor; INFERRED on a box, which this port has not
  driven -- see chapter 24.

  LAW 18f (the cursor is non-destructive).  Mouse cursor drawing backs up the
  background it covers into cursor_saved[16*16]; erasing restores those exact
  words, so there is no smear and no repaint of the region.  Under the GS
  backend this is the canvas's own rule; inside a GL layer the same argument
  is increment 6's textured quad (15, LAW 15d).

19. Desktop, compositor, input mapping
--------------------------------------

  LAW 19 (the compositor is one shared model; a PS2 fix that lands only on PS2
  has not landed).  src/window/wnd.c, src/desktop/*.c and
  src/graphics/dp_core.c are linked by the Pi 400, the hosted builds and the
  PS2 alike, so every change to them is made in the shared layer and verified
  against the arm64 target -- the standing non-regression gate is `make
  arm64-elf` after every PS2 round, and a PS2-only behaviour enters through
  #if defined(BTRON_PS2_TARGET) with a row proving the guarded branch is the
  one that shipped (chapter 22).

  LAW 19b (the canvas's content is decided by rules, and the rule that fired
  is printed).  Whether a present is the whole canvas is not a fact about the
  damage but a decision, so the [XMBT] cycle row carries why= as a bit decode:
  1 a non-move event, 2 the convergence timer, 4 global menu, 8 tracker, 16
  window-manager interaction, 32 IME, 64 band-set overflow, 128 damage that
  already spanned the canvas -- and dmg= shows the band set as the app and the
  pointer left it, BEFORE any of those rules replaced it.  Without the pair,
  "why did that frame repaint 480,000 pixels" is unanswerable from a console.

  LAW 19c (a window repaints on its own validity, not on the composite's
  mode).  redraw_all_windows_clip() used to run every intersecting window's
  paint callback whenever the composite was not blit-only, and one inval_wnd()
  made the whole composite non-blit-only -- so three windows that had not
  changed cost 55 ms a frame next to the XMB's own 184 ms.  Each window's
  repaint is now gated on its own img_valid.

  MEASURE  chapter 11's before/after: comp paint= 238,860 -> 184,260 us and
           render= 333,682 -> 279,041 us, with bfn= counting the paints that
           used to run.

  LAW 19d (no heap after init is this port's open exception, and it is
  bounded, checked, and named).  INTERPRETER.txt §3.3 forbids runtime
  allocation; core_ps2.c owns a 12 MB pool in .bss (PS2_HEAP_SIZE,
  core_ps2.c:92) that the desktop allocates from while it runs.  It used to be
  a bump allocator whose kfree() returned the block to nothing, so opening one
  window per menu click consumed the pool until a later allocation failed and
  the app painted nothing.  It is a first-fit pool with real coalescing now,
  and ps2_heap_check() walks it for the invariants -- payload inside the pool,
  sizes a multiple of the 16-byte header, no cycle -- on both sides of the
  open, which is what `[BENCH] heap before/after xmb: ... pool sound` reports.
  The header is padded to 16 bytes by a reserve word and a negative-size
  typedef asserts it, because with 12 MB of .bss the image sits about 1.5 MiB
  under ps2.ld's ASSERT and a header that grew by 8 bytes would quietly eat
  that headroom.  This is recorded as a misalignment (Appendix B) and not as a
  solved problem: lane 11's per-task stacks and §3.3 pull in the same
  direction, and neither is satisfied.

    3  the double-precision FPU ops of LAW 6a, which did nothing at all and

  LAW 19e (an app row is a WEAK opener, so a target without the app renders
  the row and does nothing).  The tracker's blocks are, in order: the live
  visible-window list, the application rows 端末 (Terminal) and 横断メディア メニュー
  (XMB), and the system rows.  The openers are weak declarations in
  include/btron/apps.h, which is why the launcher is linkable on a target that
  links no app -- and why the image gate that names an opener must ask the
  right symbol: chapter 20's table has a row that fails for exactly this
  reason.

  LAW 19f (the port obeys the Input applet, not a constant of its own).  The
  PS2 keyboard has NO repeat constants.  It reads the three values
  src/settings/input.c:16-18 defines for every target -- g_kbd_repeat_enabled,
  g_kbd_repeat_delay_us, g_kbd_repeat_interval_us -- shipped as *FX 11 = 16 cs
  (160,000 us before the first repeat) and *FX 12 = 40 cps (25,000 us between
  repeats), repeat ON; and it reads them LIVE, so the XMB's Input page changes
  what a held key does with no rebuild.  core_arm64.c:2703-2705 has always
  obeyed the same three on the Pi 400.  What the PS2 lacked was the READER,
  not the setting, and the fix creates no fourth copy of the numbers -- LAW 19
  applied to an input detail small enough to be forgotten.

  WHY  posix pays nothing for its repeat.  The host makes it, and SDL forwards
       every host repeat as another SDL_KEYDOWN (src/window/event.c:246),
       which get_evt() re-emits as another EV_KEY_DOWN.  The PS2 has no host
       under it, and its HID decoder is a six-slot differential
       (ps2_usb_process_keyboard_report(), src/drivers/ps2/ps2_usb.c:1444)
       that fires on edges only: a key held for a second is exactly two
       events, one press and one release.  The emulator's own repeat is
       unreachable, because PCSX2 hands over a DEVICE and not the host's
       keyboard settings.  So the typematic stream is produced inside the
       port, by the pass, under chapter 12's handover -- and asking the human
       to tap is not a fix.

  MECHANICS  ps2_kbd_arm(), ps2_kbd_disarm() and ps2_kbd_repeat_tick() in
             src/cores/core_ps2.c.  Three rules are load-bearing, and each is
             a bug avoided rather than a style:
  - ARMED ONLY WHEN ROUTED.  ps2_usb_on_key() swallows some chords for the
    compositor; a swallowed chord must not arm, or every interval would move
    the cursor or change the gain.
  - ONE KEY REPEATS: THE NEWEST STILL DOWN.  A later press re-arms the delay
    and the armed key's own release disarms, while releasing any OTHER key
    leaves it running.  That is what a host keyboard does, and the multi-key
    case is not what auto-repeat exists for.
  - THE TICK RUNS AFTER THE POLL AND BEFORE THE DISPATCH, in ps2_gui_pass()
    and in the Stage-1 loop.  The pass that could have seen the press is the
    pass that owes the repeat, and a release heard by this poll disarms before
    it can fire once more.  Stage 1 arms only btron_key < 0x100, because its
    prompt consumes characters and the codes above it have no binding there.
    On the GUI side a repeat is a PRESS WITH NO MATCHING RELEASE: the key
    really is still down, and a typematic stream from hardware is a run of
    downs ending in one up.

  LAW 19g (a tick pays every interval that elapsed).  A tick can run once per
  pass, and a pass is not 25 ms: this machine's measured XMB periods are
  141,519, 138,936 and 149,304 us (chapter 11).  One repeat per pass would
  therefore make the SETTING the frame rate -- the applet's 40 cps row and its
  12.5 cps row would feel identical, which is the opposite of having the
  setting.  So ps2_kbd_due() divides the elapsed gap by the interval, advances
  the schedule by the intervals it PAID and never by the clock, which is what
  keeps the MEAN rate the setting's rate, and caps the burst at
  PS2_KBD_REPEAT_MAX = 8 so that a long stall cannot flood the 256-slot event
  queue.  A gap longer than the cap can pay is DROPPED rather than banked: the
  schedule resynchronises instead of repeating at the cap forever.  And with
  no timebase the folded clock of chapter 8 reads a constant zero, every
  difference reads zero, due is zero, and repeat stays off -- a port with no
  clock does not invent a cadence.

  MEASURE  two rows, one build, 2026-10-13, from the run whose own [CFG] row
           names PS2_FP=hw:cop1 PS2_GL=eegs:

    [KBD] auto-repeat: enabled=1  delay=160000 us  interval=25000 us

    defaults           PROVEN -- the [KBD] boot row

    an unpaid backlog is dropped, not banked, and the mean rate is the

20. The /SYS volume inside the executable
-----------------------------------------

  sc and tv run INSIDE the Terminal window: gterm_execute_cmd() derives a
  row/column count from the window's own client box and calls
  sc_session_init() (src/apps/gterm.c:372-406), which paints into the cell
  grid term_get_cell() reads back in paint_gterm.  No tty device is on that
  path, and the 12 MiB heap comfortably holds the ~2.7 MiB GTermState, the
  ~640 KiB TermContext and the ScContext.  The one thing that stops it is
  storage: sc_session_init() returns at its first statement, `if (vfs_init() <
  0) return -1;` (src/clu/sc/sc.c:175), and vfs_init() on this target is one
  expression (src/clu/vfs.c:35) asking whether ANY volume is mounted.  B-MSX's
  Games band comes from vfs_list_dir() over /SYS/msx, so a machine with no
  volume gets an emulator with no cartridges for exactly the reason it gets a
  terminal with no sc.

  LAW 20 (one volume, one builder, one manifest).  /SYS is btron_sys.vol -- 33
  manifest records in 2 drawers, 26 carrying a payload, in a 1 MiB volume --
  written by src/tools/mkbtronfs.c from src/tools/manifest.txt by the Makefile
  rule that already exists for it, the same image the hosted targets mount and
  make test-fs reads.  The PS2 build adds NO second description of that
  content: no file list, no sizes, no names.

  LAW 20b (the volume rebuilds when its content changes, which means the
  manifest's payload paths are PREREQUISITES).  make otherwise rebuilds
  nothing when doc/md/PS2.md changes and the image ships the volume as it was
  last built -- the same stale-artifact disease chapter 4 and chapter 21
  document for PS2_GL and PS2_FP, arrived at from the other side: here the
  CONTENT changed, not the flag.  Two things had to be true for the sentence
  to be normative: the prerequisite list is emitted by the tool that reads the
  manifest (`--deps btron_sys.vol.d`, pulled in by -include beside the rule)
  and not by an awk one-liner in the Makefile, because that one-liner was a
  second model of the manifest's grammar -- it had to agree about quoting,
  about the `-` placeholder and about which of `FILE "name" BIN payload
  drawer`'s four fields is the path, and any one disagreement fails as a
  volume that quietly never rebuilds.  And neither mkbtronfs nor btron_sys.vol
  may be .PHONY, because a phony target has no timestamp for prerequisites to
  be measured against -- so the rule listed payloads it could not be gated by
  and rebuilt the volume every build instead.  MEASURE, on this build:
  touching assets/msx/Lode Runner (1984)(Sony)[a].rom rebuilds the volume AND
  the image; touching src/cores/core_ps2.c does not touch either.

  LAW 20c (the volume is a function of its inputs, not of the clock).  Every
  header stamp mkbtronfs used to write was time(NULL), so two runs over the
  same manifest produced different bytes -- harmless while the volume was a
  file you boot from, and a gate-breaker once it is .incbin'd: make ps2 runs
  the tool again, so the volume is always newer than the ELF's copy, and the
  row that compares them then matches an older generation against a newer one
  and reports 90 differing bytes at 0x1404 for a build that changed nothing.
  Therefore btron_fs_now_stamp() (src/fs/file.c) is the ONLY reading of "now"
  a volume takes -- vol.c's root header and file.c's create/close stamps both
  go through it, which also fixes the older bug of two time(NULL) calls
  straddling a tick and giving one volume two dates -- and
  btron_fs_set_stamp() pins it.  mkbtronfs pins before writing its first
  header, from SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH if the build exports one, otherwise from the
  newest mtime among the manifest and the payloads it names.  When no usable
  stamp can be read -- a tarball whose mtimes predate 2000-01-01 and no
  SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH -- the tool SAYS so on stderr and leaves the clock in,
  because the failure this law exists to prevent is a non-reproducible image
  that looks reproducible.  MEASURE: two runs two seconds apart are
  cmp-identical, and the volume rows are green on both.

  LAW 20d (delivery is inside the executable).  src/drivers/ps2/ps2_sys_vol.s
  pulls the volume into the link with `.incbin` and `.balign 1024` -- 1024
  because that is BTRON_BLOCK_SIZE, so every offset blk_mem_create()'s
  callbacks reach is a whole number of aligned blocks and no volume copy is an
  unaligned MIPS load.  It lands in .data (`.section .data.ps2_sys_vol, "aw"`
  matches the linker script's `*(.data.*)`, ps2.ld:38), which is writable RAM
  on this machine, so mkf, del and ins_rec work and vol_sync() writes back
  into the in-RAM copy; what does NOT survive is a reboot, which re-extracts
  the shipped image.  The ISO needed no new rule: $(PS2_ISO) copies BTRON.ELF,
  so the disc that boots the kernel already carries the volume.  The 1 MiB is
  the tail of .data and .bss starts exactly where the volume ends, so _end
  grew by the volume's own size -- and chapter 4's budget is what makes that a
  fact worth printing rather than a shrug.

  LAW 20e (the mount is the boot's, and the row that describes it is CALLED).
  btron_core_hfds_log() is that function on every target, and on this one it
  was neither called nor true: nothing in the PS2 image reached it --
  btron_kernel_init() (src/cores/core_init.c:21) is its only caller and lives
  in a TU linked here but entered only from src/desktop/main.c, which is in
  COMMON_SRCS and not in PS2_SRCS -- and its single row asserted three record
  names that are in no manifest.  It now mounts the embedded image
  (blk_mem_create -> vol_mount -> g_sys_vol) and prints name, block size,
  total and free blocks, all read back from vol_* accessors rather than
  restated.  The boot calls it as its storage step, before the banner, so
  startx, the SIO shell's CLU commands and the Terminal window all see the
  same mounted volume.  A failed
  mount is a [FS  ] row naming which of the three calls failed, because the
  failure this section exists to prevent is the SILENT one: sc falling back to
  clu_sc()'s stub line and looking like a missing feature.

  Why not the disc: a CD9660 reader is the right long-term answer for content
  a user burns, and not the cheap one.  The PS2's CDVD is an IOP peripheral,
  so an EE-side read goes MODLOAD -> LoadModule("cdvdman") -> SIF RPC -- a
  second processor's module loader and an RPC client, none of it present in a
  port whose rule is LAW 17, and none of it testable against the ELF-only
  launch path the bench runs.  The two routes differ only in where the bytes
  come from: both end at blk_mem_create over a buffer, so a CDVD reader added
  later changes the source of the image and not this section.  (Lane 13 is
  what makes the disc an EE-cheap answer rather than an EE-expensive one.)

  LAW 20f (a cartridge is a body, and ONE function reads bodies).  The four
  MSX payloads and two drawers are typed BIN, which mkbtronfs writes as
  RT_DATABOX and NOT RT_TADDATA: the type field is a reader's instruction, and
  RT_TADDATA tells the next reader that walks the record chain to decode it as
  text.  A ROM is opaque bytes, so it must be typed as opaque bytes -- rd_rec
  hands back either, and nothing complains until a body with a 0x00 in it
  comes back truncated.  The read path's half of the law: vfs_read_blob(path,
  buf, cap, &out_bytes) (src/clu/vfs.c:212) is the ONE function that moves a
  body, vfs_read_file and vfs_copy are both derived from it, and nothing reads
  a cartridge through a text wrapper -- a body whose length equals its buffer
  cannot come through vfs_read_file, which reserves the last byte for the
  terminator, and rd_rec clamps every read to the record's own size.
  src/apps/msx_app.c therefore asks for MSX_ROM_SIZE + 1 bytes: the extra byte
  is what tells a full 32 KiB page, the only thing this core maps, from a
  megaROM that would otherwise be silently cut in half, and it does so without
  a second size query because there is none -- a size comes from rd_dir, not
  from an open file.  The listing carries each record's fid beside its name
  (VfsEntry.fid) and the path handed to the reader is rebuilt through
  vfs_child_path, which re-attaches that fid as the path's `#<fid>` anchor:
  two records with the same name in the same drawer stay two different
  cartridges.

  LAW 20g (where a file lives is named in ONE place, and it is not the
  launcher's header).  MSX_BIOS_DEFAULT_DIR and MSX_ROM_CATALOGUE
  (include/emulators/tronmsx.h) are the only two places in the tree that say
  where C-BIOS and the cartridges are: third_party/openMSX/Contrib/cbios and
  assets/msx on a host, /SYS/cbios and /SYS/msx on a machine.  The bug this
  law closes was the PS2 port's first: the Games band listed both ROMs, Cross
  opened B-MSX, and the window answered "C-BIOS が読み込めません" while the BIOS sat
  in the volume -- because src/emulators/msx/tronmsx.c initialised s_bios_dir
  to the HOST folder, so the core always handed bios_load() a value, and
  bios_load() only consults the default when it is empty.

  The volume's budget, read off the image: a volume that uses 732 blocks is
  not an empty formatted volume -- it holds 33 records and their index -- and
  blk_mem_create was given readonly = 0, so the writes mkf performs go
  somewhere.  292 blocks are left, and the 114,688 bytes of media cost 127 of
  them, so the headroom is about eight more 32 KiB cartridges before the
  link's ASSERT(_end <= 0x01FF0000) starts to be argued with.  THE VOLUME IS
  THE SMALLER OF THIS IMAGE'S TWO BUDGETS, which is the sentence chapter 4's
  arithmetic was written to make possible.

PART SEVEN -- BUILDING, VERIFYING, RUNNING
=====================================================================

  A spec that cannot be rebuilt is a story.  This Part states the dials that
  select what gets built, the gate that reads the artifact back, the machines
  it has been run on, and the honest list of what none of them settles.

21. Build dials and their stamps
--------------------------------

  LAW 21 (every dial is a stamp, and every gate is handed the dial).  A build
  flag that selects code must be recorded in a file whose CONTENT names the
  value the objects were built with, and the verification step must receive
  the same value as the build.  Neither half is bookkeeping: each exists
  because an image was once graded for a pipeline it did not contain.

  THE FLAG YOU DID NOT CHANGE IS STILL THE FLAG YOU CHANGED.  make tracks file
  timestamps, not variable values, so a source that has not been edited stays
  older than its object and the object of the PREVIOUS dial value is linked
  into the next image.  Three symptoms of that disease are documented in this
  port, one per dial, and the fix is the same shape every time:

    PS2_GL      .build/ps2-gl.stamp, compared by content; the three objects
                that read the backend are deleted when it disagrees (chapter
                14).  That parse-time shell runs on EVERY make, including one
                that names no PS2 target: measured 2026-10-13, 36 s after the
                eegs image passed 91/91 the stamp read tinygl again and those
                three objects were gone -- a host-side round (`make -k test`
                or `make arm64-elf`, either is enough) had reset the dial
                while the ELF on disk went on describing eegs, so the next
                plain `make ps2` would have relinked the other pipeline under
                a book quoting these rows.  The third time a stamp in this
                port has had to explain itself, and the reason the gate below
                is handed its dial rather than run bare.
    PS2_FP      .build/ps2-fp.stamp, and PS2_FP_OBJS =
                src/drivers/ps2/ps2_builtins.ps2.o
                src/gl/tinygl/vertex_stage.ps2.o src/apps/xmb_wave.ps2.o --
                the only three units built with -mhard-float plus
                PS2_FP_CSR_COST, keyed by one substitution (PS2_CC := $(subst
                -msoft-float,-mhard-float,$(PS2_CC))) so the law has one home.
                This dial is a LEVER, not a flag: it is shared with
                ps2_builtins.c, so PS2_FP=soft withdraws COP1 from the whole
                soft-float runtime, which measured 500,386 us a frame rather
                than the ~74 ms the absence of row 7 predicted.  Stamp note
                worth knowing: $(shell ...) runs while make PARSES, so even
                `make -n PS2_FP=hw` deletes those three objects.  A dry run of
                this target is not free, and this is the second time in this
                port a stamp has had to explain itself.
    BENCH_APP   a target-specific flag on src/cores/core_ps2.pbench.o alone.
                It does not reach the ELF unless the object is rebuilt: the
                bench phase list lives in core_ps2.c, so `touch
                src/cores/core_ps2.c` (or `rm -f src/cores/core_ps2.pbench.o`)
                is part of the loop, and a missing diagnostic row means the
                flag never compiled in -- not that behaviour changed.
    PS2_FP_COUNT compiled in with -DBTRON_PS2_FP_COUNT=1 and costing about
                12.4 ms a frame, which is reading R3: no timing in this book
                comes from a counted image and no count from an uncounted one.
    HIDTRACE    -DBTRON_HID_TRACE=$(HIDTRACE), default 0: the per-keypress,
                per-report and per-control-transfer rows, including the `[KBD]
                rep k=.. due=..` line LAW 19f needs to see a repeat fire.  It
                carries NO stamp, and it is in PS2_CFLAGS for every unit, so
                toggling it is precisely the flag-you-did-not-change case
                above: make judges the objects current and the old setting
                ships.  Treat it as diagnostic-only -- `rm -f
                src/cores/core_ps2.ps2.o` when it moves -- and read its
                ABSENCE as R1 says: the dial was off, not the event did not
                happen.
    SMOOTH_*    scripts/ps2_smooth.sh deletes *.ps2.o and *.pbench.o when a
                dial changes, and takes SMOOTH_APP / SMOOTH_FRAMES /
                SMOOTH_IDLE / SMOOTH_FP_COUNT.  scripts/ps2_bench.sh runs
                PCSX2 -nogui -batch on btron-ps2-bench.elf and stops at the
                `[BENCH] run complete` sentinel; BENCH_TIMEOUT defaults to 300
                s, which truncates a slow run before the phase-closing rows
                print, so 460 is the working floor.

  THE GATE MUST BE HANDED THE SAME VALUE AS THE BUILD -- `make test-ps2
  PS2_GL=eegs` -- because test-ps2 depends on the image and would otherwise
  rebuild it back to the default and grade a different artifact.  That is not
  a style rule: it is how the first attempt at the dial bound passed while
  believing it was testing the eegs image.

    /opt/homebrew/opt/llvm/bin/clang --target=mipsel-unknown-elf \
        -march=mips2 -mabi=32 -msoft-float -ffreestanding -nostdlib -O2
    mipsel-linux-gnu-ld -T src/drivers/ps2/ps2.ld <objects> -o btron-ps2.elf

22. Verification, and how a gate earns trust
--------------------------------------------

  LAW 22 (a gate is trusted when it has been shown able to go red).  Every
  claim in this book carries a MEASURE and a GATE, and the GATE is worthless
  until somebody breaks the law on purpose and watches that exact row fail.

  is why they are quarantined in PS2-GS-LOG.md rather than dropped: a failure
  count is the only evidence that a green suite is an instrument and not a
  decoration.

  THE ROW THAT WAS RED, AND WHAT MAKING IT GREEN TOOK.  The assertion `a
  request for the GS backend installs the linked software rasterizer`
  (verify/tests/test_xmb_render.c, in test_backend_choice_round_trip) was
  written at increment 1, when the EEGS case in gl_init() was a placeholder
  that resolved to the build's default.  LAW 15a then made activation install
  the LINKED pipeline and NAME ITSELF, and increment 5c made that case
  reachable, so in the build that links TinyGL the honest answer is
  GL_BACKEND_EEGS -- the row contradicted the very law it had been written to
  hold.  It was never a defect in an image; it was a stale expectation in a
  gate, and it is now closed (2026-10-13): the contract is stated as a rule
  over the define, in the idiom the function already used for its other three
  checks.  A build that links src/gl/tinygl/ asserts gl_active_backend() ==
  GL_BACKEND_EEGS; a build that does not asserts its own software default,
  because the request there is answered by the linked software rule and takes
  its name -- and the fallback is to that rule rather than to
  gl_backend_default() on purpose, since on a PS2_GL=eegs build the default IS
  this case and the answer would call itself.  Both arms then read 75 / 75,
  and the split has teeth: writing s_active_backend = GL_BACKEND_TINYGL in the
  EEGS case makes the TinyGL arm report exactly one failure, `[FAIL] a build
  that links the pipeline answers the GS request under its own name`, and
  leaves the virgl arm at 75 / 75 because that arm takes the other branch.
  Two arms of one row disagreeing about one sentence is the instrument
  working, not a quirk -- it is what proves the expectation is a rule over the
  build rather than a constant copied from a log.

  Two facts about it a reader should keep:

  1  It stayed invisible because the aggregate is blind to it -- neither
     test-xmb-render nor test-xmb-render-tgl appears in the `test:`
     prerequisite list (Makefile:2419), so `make -k test` prints "ALL B-SYSTEM
     TEST SUITES PASSED (100% SUCCESS)" over a red row.  That is the same
     failure mode the record already caught once, from the other side:
     test-ps2-gs-rec was added to .PHONY and to `test:` BEFORE its rule
     existed, where make printed "Nothing to be done" and still exited 0 -- a
     suite that passes by not running.
  2  Changing what a gate expects IS changing the law.  That is why it is
     stated here, arm by arm, rather than quietly patched: a green suite whose
     expectation was edited is worth nothing unless the edit is named and
     shown red on demand.

  The harness traps, each earned by a wasted control run:

  - A POSITIVE CONTROL MUST SURVIVE PROVING ITSELF.  Under `set -o pipefail`,
    piping readelf/objdump into `grep -q` makes grep exit at its first match,
    the producer die of SIGPIPE, the pipeline status become 141, and a PRESENT
    thing report as missing.  Same trap, same shape: an awk that extracts one
    function's disassembly and `exit`s at the blank line SIGPIPEs the
    disassembler on its way out, and scripts/test_ps2.sh -- which runs under
    pipefail -- dies before any row prints, with no message naming the row
    that caused it.  Stop PRINTING at the range's end instead of exiting;
    print the range and read it.
  - RESTORING A SOURCE IS PART OF THE CONTROL.  A control that edits a file,
    shows the [FAIL], and puts the file back must leave the source NEWER than
    the object built from the broken version: `cp -p` restores the older
    timestamp, make judges the object up to date, and the suite re-reports the
    injected failure as though it were still in the source.  Reproduced
    deliberately (patched -> [FAIL] scissor; `cp -p` restore -> the same
    [FAIL] on a clean source; rm the object -> 35/0).  Prefer `touch`, and
    treat a failure that survives a restore as a stale object until a rebuild
    says otherwise.
  - ONE CONTROL PER COMMAND.  Several controls in one compound command let a
    later one read a source an earlier one never restored: control C's run
    printed control B's [FAIL] xyoffset, which looks like a control that does
    not work and is really a run against a dirty tree.  A control whose [FAIL]
    names a DIFFERENT law than the one it edited has measured nothing.
  - INTRA-TU CALLS ARE NOT ASSERTABLE AS jal.  ps2_gs_gif_send() lives in the
    same TU as its caller, so -O2 inlines it and the disassembly shows the
    channel-2 register dance with no jal to the sender's label.  Assert what
    survives inlining -- the DMAC window the path writes.  The call that IS
    guaranteed real is the one crossing a TU boundary, because PS2_CFLAGS
    carries no -flto.
  - A ROW THAT COUNTS DEFINITIONS CANNOT CONVICT A MISSING USE.  Deleting the
    eegs_present_get_stats() call failed NOTHING at all, because the row
    counted `<eegs_present_*>` definitions: a present that stages, sends and
    never reads its own counters still passed, and the bug it would have
    passed is the one this port has chased twice -- a [GSTS] row of zeros read
    as "the frame drew nothing" when the frame drew fine and nobody looked.
    The row now requires the READ as well, and the same mutation reports `(4
    eegs_present_* functions, 0 stats read) [FAIL]`.  That control changed a
    gate rather than the code, which is the highest-value kind of control
    there is.
  - STATIC AND INLINED means the slice has to widen.  ps2_paint_region is
    static and -O2 folds it into its caller, so row 55's counts are taken over
    both spellings and every one of them is measured inside <ps2_paint_bands>.
  - A SCAN MUST BE SCOPED TO A SECTION.  The row that asks whether the code
    names the checkout's paths used to scan the WHOLE image and went red on
    this file's own sentence about the bug -- 2 hits, both prose.  Locating
    .rodata by section Addr/Off and scanning only that window is what makes
    the row read code rather than documentation.
  - THE FOLDER IS A HARNESS TOO, AND IT READS A UNIT AS A PRONOUN.  This
    book's prose is folded to 78 columns by .build/rewrap_book2.py, which
    tells a sentence from a figure by counting English function words -- and
    `us` is one of them, so `draw=49277 us` sitting at a display indent is
    English to the tool.  Three separate [ASYNC] rows once came out of it as
    one sentence.  The damage is invisible in a read-through because every
    word survives, only re-spaced: what is lost is grepability, which is the
    whole reason a console row is quoted.  A bracketed row name deeper than
    the body, set off by a blank line or hanging under a deeper continuation,
    is now a record and passes through byte for byte.  The one gate that
    cannot lie about a fold is the tool's own token multiset: the words in and
    the words out must be IDENTICAL, which is what this book's 2026-10-13
    source reports.  A fold that drops or duplicates a word fails that check
    before a reader has to find the difference by eye.
  - THE SAME FOLDER CAN EAT A LABEL'S GUTTER, AND THE TOKEN CHECK IS BLIND TO
    THAT TOO.  A record's text gets re-filled on its own because its label row
    is a column, and the rule is that the label and its gutter never move --
    but the gutter was being rebuilt from the DIFFERENCE between the cell's
    column and its continuations', so a label one column narrower than its own
    block came back with one space where the book's idiom says two, and `Gate
    bg=` shipped with its text a column LEFT of the block it opens.  Not one
    word moved.  The text column is now whichever sits further out -- the
    continuations' column or the label plus two spaces -- which is what aligns
    chapter 16's five LANE heads; the alignment is a rule, not an eye.

  And the law that governs every claim above: SKIP, not PASS, for an assertion
  this image did not actually exercise.  `make all` was at five of seven
  targets when increment 5c landed, and foma's link break is reported rather
  than fixed -- `clu.foma.o` wants open_xmb_window, open_glgears_window and
  open_lilcu64_demo_window and FOMA_SRCS lists none of those files.  A target
  that did not link is not a target that passed.

23. Running: PCSX2, MIPS, toolchain, quick reference
----------------------------------------------------

  Three machines carry this port, and they answer different questions.

  PCSX2 (Target 8).  The image runs under a REPOSITORY-LOCAL data path --
  `PCSX2_DATADIR ?= $(CURDIR)/pcsx2`, config at pcsx2/PCSX2/inis/PCSX2.ini --
  so the emulator's knobs are versioned with the port instead of living in
  ~/Library/Application Support.  ps2-cfg checks that ini and the BIOS
  directory (PCSX2_BIOS, defaulting to ~/Library/Application
  Support/PCSX2/bios) and creates bios/ logs/ memcards/ sstates/ snaps/
  cache/, symlinking each BIOS .bin rather than copying it.  Then:

    make run-ps2          direct ELF, -fastboot       (the default)
    make run-ps2 ISO=1    btron-ps2.iso as CDVD media
    make test-ps2         the static image gate

  Two facts about the emulator matter to the book's measurements:
  - -fastboot skips Sony's 7-sound intro and boots straight into the kernel,
    and the ISO route exists partly so that double-clicking a .elf never
    becomes this machine's habit: PCSX2's Info.plist declares itself the
    handler for public.filename-extension elf, which is why every other .elf
    on the Mac (btron-foma.elf, btron-mips.elf) shows a PCSX2 icon.  To undo
    it:
      /System/Library/Frameworks/CoreServices.framework/Frameworks/\
          LaunchServices.framework/Support/lsregister -r -domain local \
          -domain system -domain user
      killall Finder
      brew install duti && duti -s com.apple.TextEdit .elf all
    and the Makefile's own route avoids LaunchServices by exec'ing
    /Applications/PCSX2.app/Contents/MacOS/PCSX2 (or `open --args -datapath
    ...`).
  - The renderer should be Metal on Apple Silicon (lowest latency, native host
    acceleration for the Host-to-Local GIF DMA); Vulkan or Software is for
    verifying pixel-exact GS eDRAM swizzling without host GPU filtering.
    Frame Limiting at Normal (100%) when frame cadence looks wrong.  For real
    input,
    Settings -> Controllers -> USB: Port 1 standard HID Mouse, Port 2 standard
    HID Keyboard -- which is what makes the `hw` pointer profile of chapter 13
    reachable at all.
  - PointerXScale / PointerYScale in that ini look like a gain dial and are
    inert: neither name exists in PCSX2 v2.8.2's source for a USB
    Type::Pointer binding.  There is ONE gain constant in this stack,
    PS2_EMU_MULT_FP, and chapter 13's ptgain is how it is checked.

  Bare-metal MIPS (Target 9), QEMU, for the parts that are architecture rather
  than Emotion Engine:

    make mips             btron-mips.elf
    make run-mips         QEMU Cocoa window + serial console (Ctrl+Alt+G frees
                          the mouse); make run-mips GUI=0 or make test-mips
                          for headless
    Malta Core LV         qemu-system-mipsel -M malta -cpu 24Kf; 256 MB RAM at
                          KSEG0 0x80000000; entry 0x80100000; NS16550 COM1 at
                          physical 0x180003F8 / KSEG1 0xB80003F8, 115200 8N1
    Magnum R4000          qemu-system-mips64el -M magnum -bios ./ntprom.raw -m
                          64M -- needs the ARC firmware image in the repo root

  Both targets compile natively on the host with LLVM/Clang and GNU binutils;
  no external GCC toolchain is a prerequisite.  What the MIPS target does NOT
  provide is the GS, the OHCI host, or the R5900, so it verifies kernel boot
  markers and nothing about a frame.

  Quick reference:

    make ps2                btron-ps2.elf + btron-ps2.iso
    make run-ps2            PCSX2, direct ELF;  ISO=1 for the disc
    make test-ps2           91/91 image gate (hand the build's dial value)
    make ps2-bench          the bench ELF;  BENCH_APP=1 BENCH_TIMEOUT=460
                            scripts/ps2_bench.sh for the headless loop
    scripts/ps2_smooth.sh   per-phase budget gates (SMOOTH_APP/FRAMES/IDLE)
    make mips / run-mips / test-mips          Target 9
    make arm64-elf          the Pi 400 -- run after every PS2 round
    make -k test            every host suite in the aggregate list
    make clean              every .elf, .iso, .o and test binary

24. What is open, and what no measurement here can settle
---------------------------------------------------------

  An honest spec ends with its own limits.  Four kinds of open, kept separate
  because they need different instruments.

  A. STRUCTURAL -- the missing agents of LAW 1, i.e. lanes 9-13 (chapter 16).
     Until lane 11 lands, every budget in this book is a number a body may
     exceed with the kernel unable to notice, and the correct description of
     the design remains ONE PREEMPTION POINT PER FRAME, CHOSEN BY THE BODY.
     Until lane 10 lands, VIOLATION 2 stands and `snd` on the presentation
     path is a spin.

  B. IMPLEMENTED, NOT YET CLOSED on screen:
     - increment 6, the pointer and the window decorations as GS sprites.
       Inside a GL layer's rect the pointer is currently invisible (LAW 15d)
       and nothing headless can certify a z-order, so this one's gate is the
       user's eyes.
     - one drawing environment and one frame buffer (LAW 15f): a second GL
       window opened while a layer exists draws into the FIRST window's rect.
     - glClear() is TinyGL's own fill and not a primitive, so a layer's
       background is the canvas showing through -- invisible for the XMB's
       full mesh, a real gap for an app that clears and draws less.
     - PRMODE / PRMODECONT unconfirmed, so PRIM rides every vertex and
       GS_REC_REGS_N stays 4 instead of 3 (chapter 16).  INFERRED.
     - TEX1's filter is not threaded from the app: magnification LINEAR,
       minification NEAREST, which a 1:1 UI cannot distinguish; a MINIFIED
       textured primitive is the one case the GS has no answer for without a
       mipmap chain this port does not build.
     - D2_QWC is 12-bit while the canvas chunks at 8,000 quadwords; correct
       today, unproven at the bound, and changing it moves every upload number
       here.
     - Keyboard auto-repeat (LAW 19f, 19g) is in, and its cadence law is gated
       by the [BENCH] kbd cadence row; what remains is a hand, not an
       instrument -- whether 160 ms is the right delay for a person typing,
       and a repeat that works prints NOTHING on a default image, because the
       per-burst row exists only under HIDTRACE=1.
     - Escape at the XMB root does not close the window (#30); Command/Alt +
       letter does not open and traverse the deskbar (#24).  The boot output
       names the dials the image was BUILT under -- `[CFG] PS2_FP=hw:cop1
       PS2_GL=eegs` (2026-10-13) -- but not the rasterizer gl_init() actually
       installed, and that is the half of #61 still open: LAW 14e's fallback
       is exactly the case where the two answers differ.

  C. MEASURED ON AN EMULATOR, AND ONLY THAT.  Every microsecond in this book
     is PCSX2's, and the port's own reading rule says what follows: RELATIONS
     between columns (which stage dominates, which cut moved what) are
     instruction counts and travel; ABSOLUTE values are the emulator's and do
     not.  Two specific emulator facts are load-bearing and would need
     re-measurement on a box: the clock divisor is 295 ticks/us here, not the
     documented 147, and the PCRTC geometry is keyed off CMOD/LC in a way that
     forced the 720p-mode choice (chapter 18).  The real-console check this
     book cannot perform is task #17: live clicks, hovers and pointer feel on
     hardware.  B-MSX's speed (#63) is the same class of open -- it runs, and
     it runs too slowly to play well, and that is a per-frame question owned
     by chapters 10-11, not a storage one.

  D. WHAT NO MEASUREMENT HERE CAN SETTLE AT ALL.  Whether this frame feels
     smooth to a person; whether the XMB's animation reads as motion at
     138-149 ms periods; whether a wallpaper-as-texture is worth its 132
     remaining VRAM pages.  The book's instrument is a console line and a
     pixel census, and both are silent about taste.

  And one claim this book would like to make and cannot: that the ratio of
  chapter 1.5 -- ~29x, the number that named the GS -- is the whole story.  It
  is the story of the FILL.  After increments 5 and 7 and 8, the EE frame is
  geometry, compositing and the bus, and the next 2.4x will not come from
  arithmetic at all: it comes from the compositor's 613,440-pixel blit still
  being EE work, which is exactly what lane 9 is, and what this book's final
  unfinished chapter is about.

APPENDIX A -- LABEL SCHEME, DECODED

  This port's console lines and doc rows carry short labels.  Every one of
  them, in one place, so a log can be read without archaeology:

  Plan and increment labels
    5a / 5b / 5c / 5d   presentation in steps: raster hooks / texture pages /
                        present (5c is landed) / cursor+overlays as GS sprites
                        (open, = increment 6 in the table)
    rows 7, 8           the COP1 kernels: vertex_stage.c, then xmb_wave.c
    row 9a              the PLATE REFUSAL (11.5): a layer window's flat body
                        fill, skipped over exactly the rect its own stream
                        covers -- a refusal, not a poster, and 9b is still the
                        poster
    lanes 9-13          the five open agents: GS compositor, GIF writer ring,
                        real Tier-1 tick, VU1 geometry actor, IOP/SIF I/O
                        actor
    S= / D=             the float census's single- and double-precision call
                        counts per draw function, for one frame
    why=<hex>           which rule made a present the whole canvas: 1 non-move
                        event, 2 convergence timer, 4 global menu, 8 tracker,
                        16 window manager, 32 IME, 64 band-set overflow,
                        128 damage already spanned it
    [GSTS] columns      groups / qw / verts / prims / gs | drop unsplit texsw
                        texmiss | cover coverdrop | clip b0 plate -- gs= is
                        the EE's SEND-AND-WAIT bracket, never GS pipeline time
    bfn=                window paint callbacks that used to run and now do not
    wins=<drawn/walked> how many windows the composite painted, of how many it
                        visited
    body= / skip=       9a's DECISION counts, reset at the top of the walk:
                        the px the composite drew and the px it refused, for
                        the whole frame.  Not an accumulation -- a build that
                        has refused nothing reads skip=0
    clip=               the client rect a GS layer window's environment packet
                        addresses, as W x H
    b0= / plate=        the span of the stream's FIRST vertex group, rounded
                        INWARD (a vertex at 767.4 reaches 767, never 768), and
                        the verdict on it: covered, SHORT, or none when the
                        frame recorded no vertices.  plate= is the premise of
                        the refusal, not a quality of the picture
    [GSPLATE]           one-shot warning that b0= does not span clip= -- the
                        plate has to stay drawn, so the row is the build
                        telling you a refusal was made on an untrue premise
    t1024 / dm / dsum   the wave instrument: time for 1,024 lattice steps,
                        mean per-step delta, sum of deltas (a dsum of 0 with a
                        large dm means motion that cancels -- a frozen sheet)
    LIVE / STALLED / DEAD  the clock's verdict on the 1 kHz plane over a window
    bb=1 fb=1 pre/post  band-blit and full-blit bookkeeping around a present
    px= nb= full=       pixels uploaded, band count, whether band 0 is the canvas

  GS register and packet labels
    CT32 / PSM          32-bit RGBA pixel storage mode; byte order R,G,B,A in
                        memory -- the reason chapter 7's byte-order law exists
    FBA / FBW / ZBA     frame-buffer address (8 KB units) and width; Z base
    TBA / TBW           texture base address (256 B units) and width (64-texel
                        units); TW/TH are EXPONENTS, so a 384 upload is
                        described as 512
    AD form             an address+data register write: one quadword per
                        write, which is why GS_REC_AD_QWC(n) is 1 + NLOOP x
                        NREG with NREG = 1
    FLG = 0 / 2         a GIFTAG's format: 0 PACKED (primitives), 2 IMAGE (pixels)
    NLOOP               vertices (PACKED) or quadwords (IMAGE) in a group --
                        never both, and two controls in this port failed
                        exactly on that confusion
    XYOFFSET / SCISSOR  0x8000 + window x/y for the 12.4 vertex origin;
                        SCISSOR is PLAIN unsigned screen px 0..2047, so
                        vertices are window-relative and the clip rect is not
    SPRITE / BITBLTBUF  the GS's fill and blit forms -- present in the encoder
                        table, and with no caller that blits inside VRAM (lane
                        9)

  Evidence labels (R2)
    PROVEN     measured on our own run, or read out of a register/packet table
               and reproduced by a gate
    INFERRED   believed from a datasheet, a default, or a secondary source,
               and therefore still a candidate
    SKIP       an assertion this image did not exercise -- never written as
               PASS
